## Public Profile

**Remand Health** is a venture-backed health-care company in [Tyler, Texas](/wiki/places/tyler-texas/), whose only service is being stolen from. It operates a single storefront, a former video rental store on South Broadway Avenue fitted out as a convenience store, from which its enrolled clients steal amounts calibrated to the Texas Penal Code so that they are arrested, convicted and confined in the Smith County jail for long enough to receive and recover from the medical treatment they could not otherwise obtain. The company was founded in 2025 by [Wade Castleberry](/wiki/people/wade-castleberry/), a former health-insurance prior-authorization manager, and opened its storefront in June 2026. In November 2026 it closed a $14 million Series A round led by [Fullspeed Capital](/wiki/organizations/fullspeed-capital/).

The company's legal foundation is *Estelle v. Gamble* (1976), in which the Supreme Court held that deliberate indifference to the serious medical needs of prisoners violates the Eighth Amendment. Remand's pitch deck describes the decision as "the only universal coverage mandate in American law" and its own role as "eligibility assistance."

### How the Service Works

- **Enrollment** is online and free.
- **Clinical-legal intake** is a single appointment with a nurse practitioner and a criminal defense attorney, both Remand employees, who match the client's diagnosis to an offense whose expected period of confinement covers treatment and recovery.
- **The formulary** is the company's table of those matches. A dental extraction is a Class B misdemeanor theft, typically a $140 carton of cigarettes, good for up to 180 days. A hernia repair or rotator cuff is a Class A misdemeanor theft, achieved by emptying the register, which opens each morning holding $812, which is $62 above the Class A threshold. Robbery, a second-degree felony carrying two to 20 years, is regarded by the clinical staff as overtreatment for anything short of an organ transplant and is not offered at the Broadway location.
- **Disposition:** clients plead guilty at arraignment and decline probation, which the company calls "the leading cause of loss of coverage."

The company uses the word "robbery" in its marketing. Its attorneys do not.

### Revenue

Remand does not charge its clients. Two regional hospital systems, which the company has declined to name, pay it $1,150 for each uninsured patient it moves from their emergency departments into county custody, after which the patient returns to the same hospital under guard, is treated by the same physicians, and is billed to Smith County. Mr. Castleberry describes the arrangement as "changing the payer, not the medicine."

### Consent

A theft to which the owner consents is not a theft. Remand's clerks are therefore not told which customers are enrolled, and the company's board maintains a standing resolution opposing all thefts from its premises. The company's position, in Mr. Castleberry's words, is that it "does not consent. It anticipates."

### Status

As of November 2026 the waiting list to steal from the Broadway location stood at 2,300 people and eleven weeks, shorter than the wait for a new-patient appointment at Smith County's free clinic. The company plans four further Texas locations and two in Florida in 2027, each in a county without a public hospital. Its matching engine, a model trained on the Texas Penal Code and eleven years of Smith County sentencing records, was cited by Fullspeed as one of two reasons for the investment. The other was the payer.

## Private Profile

**Function in stories:** Remand is the universe's health-care company, in the sense that it is the only one in the universe that has found a payer that cannot refuse. It is a straight-faced, well-run start-up whose business model is a fully accurate reading of American law. Nothing it does is a trick. Every step is legal, documented and priced, and the whole of it exists because a constitutional duty of care attaches to custody and to nothing else.

**Tone:** Clinical, courteous, procedural. Remand speaks the language of benefits administration: eligibility, intake, formulary, coverage, loss of coverage. It never says "crime." It says "qualifying event."

**Useful tensions:** The store's clerks do not know who is enrolled, so every ordinary customer is, to them, a possible client, and every client a customer. Smith County pays for everything and was asked about nothing. The hospitals pay Remand to send them patients they were already treating. The county clinic that would make Remand unnecessary would cost less than Remand costs the county, and has been opposed for helping people who have not done anything.

**Generative threads:** The Commissioners Court hearing. The first client who needs a transplant. The 2027 expansion into Florida. A competitor offering a better formulary. A client acquitted against his will. A clerk who works out who is enrolled. The man in the back brace.

**Avoid:** Making Remand cynical, predatory or fraudulent. Its staff believe they are helping people and they are. The comedy is that they are right.
