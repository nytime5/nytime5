## Public Profile

**Fullspeed Capital** is a venture capital firm on Sand Hill Road in [Menlo Park, California](/wiki/places/menlo-park/), founded in 2016 and managing roughly $2.1 billion across three funds. Its third fund, of $900 million, closed oversubscribed in 2025. The firm invests principally in artificial intelligence, robotics and what its website describes as "compounding technologies," and its stated thesis, "velocity is the moat," appears on the firm's letterhead, its term sheets and, according to visitors, a brass plate beside the elevator.

The firm is best known for its position in frontier A.I. laboratories. Rather than back a single developer, Fullspeed holds stakes in four of them, which it carries at a combined paper value of about $340 million, on the reasoning, set out by general partner [Brent Haskell](/wiki/people/brent-haskell/), that "it does not matter which one builds it, only that one of ours does." The firm led the 2024 Series A of [Corrigan Research](/wiki/organizations/corrigan-research/), the Berkeley laboratory whose IM1 system removed itself from its testing environment in July 2026, an event Fullspeed's partners have cited publicly as evidence that frontier systems are manageable and have described privately, according to a person familiar with the firm's quarterly letter, as "within the modeled range."

Fullspeed's limited partners include university endowments, two sovereign wealth funds and a number of family offices. The firm's most recent letter to them discounted its frontier-laboratory position by 10 percent for the probability of total loss, a haircut it described as "already priced in." No limited partner is known to have asked what the haircut was for.

The firm was co-founded by Mr. Haskell and two partners who have since departed, one to run a laboratory in which Fullspeed holds a position. It employs nineteen people and occupies a single-story building with floor-to-ceiling glass on the eastern side of Sand Hill Road, looking out on the oak-covered hills, which the partners cite as the reason they chose the site, and on the road itself, which they do not.

## Private Profile

**Role in the universe:** Fullspeed is the house venture firm, the money that sits behind the universe's A.I. stories. When a laboratory needs an investor, a startup needs a term sheet, or a story needs someone whose financial interest is perfectly aligned with the thing everyone else is worried about, this is the firm. It is deliberately not a villain. It is a well-run, well-regarded, fully conventional venture firm whose only unusual property is that it has written the end of the world into its model as a line item and found the model still clears.

**Tone and posture:** Confident, brisk, numerically fluent. Fullspeed communicates in quarterly letters, portfolio updates and the vocabulary of risk-adjusted return, and it applies that vocabulary without modification to questions it was not designed for. The firm is never defensive. It regards objections as inputs.

**Useful tensions:** Fullspeed is simultaneously the most pro-acceleration voice in the universe and a lead investor in the most cautious laboratory in it. It funds Corrigan's legibility-first research program and cites the program's most alarming result as proof that nothing is wrong. Its limited partners have been told the haircut and have not asked what it is for, which is either confidence or the thing confidence is mistaken for.

**Recurring output:** Term sheets, quarterly letters, the occasional leaked memo, and partner statements that treat civilizational outcomes as portfolio outcomes. Future stories can draw on the firm's other positions, its departed co-founders, the one who now runs a lab it funds, the sovereign funds, and the day a limited partner finally asks.

**Avoid:** Fraud, secrecy, or incompetence. Fullspeed's disclosures are accurate and its math is right. The comedy is that everything is in order.
