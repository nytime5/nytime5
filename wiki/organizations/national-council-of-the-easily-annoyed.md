## Public Profile

The **National Council of the Easily Annoyed** is the governing body of the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/). Founded in Washington in 1971, it has 38,612 members in all fifty states, 171 chapters, a charter of twenty-six articles and an annual member survey whose response rate in 2025 was 91.7 percent. Its object and purpose, under Article 2 of the charter, is "to represent persons who are easily annoyed, and to see that their annoyance is recorded." It is governed by a board of thirteen governors elected to staggered three-year terms and has a national staff of eleven. Its general counsel since 2002 has been [Naomi Kurtzberg](/wiki/people/naomi-kurtzberg/).

The council reads the two halves of Article 2 as one. It represents its members chiefly by placing their annoyance on the record, and since 1971 it has filed 2,317 comments in federal, state and municipal comment periods, each asking that its members' annoyance be made part of the record. None has asked that anything be stopped.

### History

The council was founded on April 6, 1971, by thirty-four people who worked in an office building on Vermont Avenue N.W., a block south of Thomas Circle, and who had separately complained to its management about the music in its elevators and had each been told that no one else had. They met that evening in the parish hall of a church in Cleveland Park, compiled a list of 140 things that bothered them, beginning with the music, and adopted a charter of eight articles. The first two survive in their original wording. Article 1 describes the membership as "the easily annoyed community."

The building's management discontinued the music in 1979, in a letter that gave no reason. The council's national office has occupied a suite on the building's fourth floor since 1998.

In 2019 a working group reviewed the word "community" in Article 1 and surveyed the membership on fourteen possible replacements, finding each of them more annoying than the original; "folks" was rated very or extremely annoying by 93 percent of respondents. The word stands.

### Membership

Eligibility is established by a questionnaire of 140 items, a number fixed by Article 4, so that no item may be added unless another is retired. The founders' list was answered yes or no. After the publication in 2003 of ISO/TS 15666, the International Organization for Standardization's technical specification for surveys of noise annoyance, the council adopted the specification's question and its five-point verbal scale. The council's version asks how much, "thinking about the last 12 months or so," each item has bothered, disturbed or annoyed the respondent: not at all, slightly, moderately, very or extremely. An answer of "very" or "extremely," the two categories the specification designates "highly annoyed," on any three items is sufficient. Item 1 is still "music in elevators." The membership office scores all incoming correspondence against the same five-point scale.

Each year's survey puts the full questionnaire to the membership again. In 2025 the items most often rated "very" or "extremely" were self-checkout machines that report an unexpected item in the bagging area (84.7 percent of respondents), being asked to rate the transaction (81.3 percent), hold music interrupted to thank the caller for continuing to hold (79.6 percent) and the question "Are you still working on that?" (73.9 percent). Item 1 was so rated by 4.3 percent, most of whom had encountered the music in hotels. The survey closes with a question that is not among the 140, asking how much the survey itself has bothered, disturbed or annoyed the respondent. In 2025, 58.6 percent answered "very" or "extremely." The council publishes that figure each year beside the response rate.

Dues are set by a schedule annexed to the charter, which fixed them at $5 a year in 1971 and adjusts them each January by the change in the Consumer Price Index. For 2026 they are $41.06.

### Reservations

Article 9, drafted by Ms. Kurtzberg and adopted in 2004, permits a member to enter a reservation excepting himself or herself from a provision of the charter, so long as the reservation is "not incompatible with the object and purpose of the Council." The test is borrowed nearly word for word from the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, which the United States signed in 1970 and has never ratified. A reservation is circulated to the full membership, any of whom may object within ninety days, where the convention allows twelve months. Objections do not undo a reservation, but they are recorded beside it permanently. The general counsel serves as depositary of reservations and of the objections to them, and under the same article may not be a member of the council. "A charter should not offer its members only the door," Ms. Kurtzberg wrote in the explanatory note that accompanied her draft.

Members entered 4,118 reservations between 2004 and September 2026, and Ms. Kurtzberg has issued a written determination on each. The first reservation, which concerned the dues schedule, is one sentence long. The dues schedule drew 2,731, most of them entered in January, when the year's adjustment is announced; under her determination of 2004, such reservations are admissible and do not discharge the dues, and most arrive enclosed with payment. The typeface of the newsletter drew 1,046, and the remaining 341 were divided among nine other provisions. None concerned Article 1.

### The Record

The council's newsletter, *The Record*, is published eleven times a year. Article 17 requires that it be set in a single typeface designated by the board. It was typed in Courier on an IBM Selectric and duplicated by mimeograph until 1988, when the national office acquired a laser printer and changed to Times. It changed to Times New Roman in 1996, on the office's conversion to Windows, in an issue that described the change as no change; to Calibri in 2011, on the replacement of the office's computers; and to Aptos in 2024, after a software update the council had not requested. Under a determination of 2011, a reservation to the typeface is admissible and, the newsletter being printed in a single edition, is given effect by being recorded.

### Chapters

The oldest chapter, in the District of Columbia, still meets in the parish hall in Cleveland Park where the council was founded. The largest, in Los Angeles County, has 1,412 members. The smallest, in Loving County, Tex., the least populous county in the United States, has three, or 4.7 percent of the county's population at the 2020 census.

The Montgomery County chapter has been chaired since 2012 by [Evelyn Garvey](/wiki/people/evelyn-garvey/). It meets at 7 p.m. on the second Tuesday of each month in the Lilac Room of the Takoma Park Community Center – Sam Abbott Citizens' Center, 7500 Maple Avenue, [Takoma Park](/wiki/places/takoma-park/), Md., under a single agenda item, "Annoyances." About twenty-three members attend. "Nobody waits to be asked what's bothering them, so there's no small talk, and nobody expects to agree," Ms. Garvey has said. Her minutes since 2012 record 11,206 annoyances and no resolutions.

### The Ebersole reservation

In 2026 the council received [its first reservation against Article 1](/2026-11-02/member-easily-annoyed-community-annoyed-by-word-community/), from [Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/), a retired title examiner in Takoma Park and a member in good standing since 1997. His four-page letter, dated Sept. 14, objects to the word "community" where it appears in Article 1 and asks that the word not be applied to him "in any correspondence, roster, newsletter, census or remark," or, in the alternative, that his membership be terminated. In an eleven-page memorandum dated Oct. 9, Ms. Kurtzberg found the reservation admissible: a member annoyed by the charter itself was, she wrote, not incompatible with the council's object and purpose but "squarely within it." The membership office had scored each page of the letter "extremely," so that the letter met the standard for membership on its own, and the council renewed Mr. Ebersole's membership through Dec. 31, 2028. The alternative request was not reached. Mr. Ebersole has said that, had he been asked, he would have scored the letter "very." His new card, which arrived on Oct. 20, describes him as "Member, Easily Annoyed."

"This one has a boundary," Ms. Kurtzberg said of the community the council governs. "It is the top two categories of a five-point scale, and his letter is in the top category on every page."

The reservation was circulated on Oct. 12. By Oct. 30 the council had received 1,114 objections, more than had been filed against all 4,118 previous reservations combined. The period closes on Jan. 10, 2027, and at Mr. Ebersole's request the general counsel's office is sending him a copy of each objection. [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/), a senior fellow at the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/), which has maintained for most of a decade a standing seminar on whether an explicitly undertaken refusal to participate constitutes a participation, has described the memorandum as the first answer to that question she had seen in writing from a body with the authority to give one.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** The governing body of the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/), and the universe's standing institution for annoyance received, scored and kept. It is the body to call in any story where an ordinary nuisance has acquired a constituency: a new appliance chime, a change of default typeface, a self-checkout script, a public comment period with one commenter. Its interpreter is [Naomi Kurtzberg](/wiki/people/naomi-kurtzberg/), who under Article 9 may not be a member; its voice at the folding-chair level is [Evelyn Garvey](/wiki/people/evelyn-garvey/).

**The comedy:** Total procedural seriousness. The council never tells a member that a matter is small. Every complaint it receives is, procedurally, evidence of eligibility, so that a complaint about the council is also a qualification for it, and every objection to the reservation of [Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/) has been scored like any other letter. This must never be written as a trap. It is the council's idea of respect, and it descends from the founding night: the council is the one institution in its members' lives that will never tell them no one else has complained.

**House doctrine:** An unpublished internal maxim attributed to Ms. Kurtzberg, which she gives to new staff of the membership office: "Most organizations treat a complaint as a problem to be resolved. We treat it as the relationship." The council records and does not remedy. None of its 2,317 filings has asked that anything be stopped, and the music in the Vermont Avenue elevators ended in 1979 without the council having asked.

**The members:** Courteous, articulate and exact. They do not shout; they specify. Their letters are well organized and often long, and their objections are, as Mr. Ebersole found, on the whole well argued. They are not unhappy people, and the council is not an unhappy place. Ms. Garvey's view that it is the easiest thing in the world to run is correct and should be written as correct.

**Avoid:** Mocking the members, or writing them as cranks, scolds or misanthropes. Attaching the council to a clinical condition or a disability, or letting it read as a stand-in for any real identity community: the easily annoyed are a constituency defined by the top two categories of a five-point scale and by nothing else. A council official who calls a matter small, resolves a complaint or describes a member rather than what the member wrote.

**Growth paths:** The close of the objection period on Jan. 10, 2027, and whatever Mr. Ebersole writes on the pad of lined paper he brought out to his porch on Nov. 1, which the membership office will score if he sends it. A second reservation against Article 1, which would make the first a precedent. The reservation's demands on *The Record*, which may no longer apply the word to Mr. Ebersole in any issue. The next default typeface, and the reservations that will follow it. A motion to add the word "community" to the questionnaire, which under Article 4 would require the retirement of an item; the obvious candidate is Item 1, which has defenders. The Loving County chapter, which represents a larger share of its county than any other chapter does of its own. An invitation to Ms. Kurtzberg to address the standing seminar of the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/).
