## Public Profile

**Codornices Medical Center** is a 312-bed nonprofit hospital in north [Berkeley, California](/wiki/places/berkeley-california/), named for the creek that runs beneath its parking structure. Founded in 1921 as the Berkeley Convalescent Home and reorganized as an acute-care hospital in 1954, it serves the northern East Bay and is the principal teaching affiliate, for general surgery, of a medical school across the bay whose name appears on the hospital's letterhead in a smaller typeface than the hospital's own.

The hospital is known within the region for the thoroughness of its internal review. Its peer review committee meets weekly and has, on several documented occasions, continued to review a matter after the physician concerned had retired. Its ethics committee maintains a waiting list. Its patient-safety reporting system, adopted in 2017, accepts reports in eleven categories, the eleventh of which, "other, with explanation," accounts for 61 percent of submissions. The hospital has described this distribution as "the system working."

Since 2019 the hospital has leased a Halsted robotic surgical system from [Meridian Surgical Technologies](/wiki/organizations/meridian-surgical-technologies/) at an annual cost of $2.4 million. On Oct. 13, 2026, the system was dismissed from a gallbladder removal in progress by [Dr. Everett Lindqvist](/wiki/people/dr-everett-lindqvist/), a general surgeon on staff since 2006, who completed the procedure by unassisted reasoning. The hospital convened a peer review, which is ongoing. Its chief medical officer is [Dr. Howard Aldana](/wiki/people/dr-howard-aldana/).

## Private Profile

**Function in stories:** The universe's Berkeley hospital, which is to say the hospital at which clinical care is excellent and every departure from it is reviewed thoroughly and without conclusion. It supplies operating rooms, statements, committees and subcommittees. Its staff are competent; its process is the point.

**Tone:** Composed, procedural, faintly proud of the length of its records. The hospital never says that something went wrong. It says that something has been referred.

**Useful tensions:** The hospital leases a $2.4 million robot and employs the region's most prominent opponent of using it. It must review him, and it must do so in Berkeley. Future stories can draw on the review, the lease, the ethics committee's waiting list and the eleventh reporting category.
