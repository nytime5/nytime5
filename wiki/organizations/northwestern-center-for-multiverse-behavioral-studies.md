## Public Profile

The **Northwestern Center for Multiverse Behavioral Studies** is a research institute at Northwestern University that studies human behavior across adjacent realities. It operates one of the few working multiverse apparatuses in the United States, an instrument that simulates parallel versions of the world in order to vary a single condition, such as the society a child is raised in, while holding all others constant. Researchers elsewhere describe the apparatus as "expensive to operate," and the Center spends a considerable share of each fiscal year securing the funds to switch it on.

The Center's research program rests on the transplant study, in which the same subject is raised under different conditions across many realities and the outcomes compared. Its most widely reported result, by senior fellow [Dr. Harlan Speece](/wiki/people/dr-harlan-speece/), found across 847 simulated realities that 95 percent of people raised among cannibals would become cannibals. The study appeared in the *Proceedings of the Institute for Parallel Anthropological Sciences*, and outside scholars called its conclusion "not entirely surprising" while praising its rigor.

The apparatus has a physical aperture, which during calibration runs occasionally admits small quantities of printed matter from adjacent realities. The Center logs, sleeves and archives every page it receives. Its holdings include grocery circulars, tide tables and, since September 2026, a 2010 column by a writer named Christopher Hitchens, which the Center's stylometric software matched so closely to the work of the *New York Time5* columnist [Kristoffer Kitchens](/wiki/people/kristoffer-kitchens/) that staff forwarded it to him. The aperture works in one direction only. The Center can receive documents from adjacent realities and has no means of replying.

## Private Profile

**Role in the universe:** The Center is this world's working evidence that the multiverse exists, and it treats that fact as a budget line rather than a revelation. It produces findings about parallel selves with the same institutional calm a university brings to soil samples, and it is the natural explanation for any document, data point or comparison that arrives from another reality.

**Useful for:** Stories about the multiverse, nature versus nurture, counterfactual selves, and any occasion on which a character in this world needs to encounter the work of their counterpart in another. The Center will supply the document. It will not comment on what it means.
