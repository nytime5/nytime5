## Public Profile

The **Center for Cohort Boundary Research** is an independent demographic research institute in [Madison, Wisconsin](/wiki/places/madison-wisconsin/), dedicated to the study of where American generations begin and end, who decided, and when. It was founded in 2004 by a group of University of Wisconsin–Madison demographers who had, in the words of its founding statement, "noticed that nobody was in charge of this." Its relationship to the university is described in its own documentation as "adjacent."

The Center does not assign people to generations. It maintains the Concordance, a reconciliation table of thirty-one published generational frameworks, from the Census Bureau's definition of the baby boom to the marketing industry's most recent proposals, which records, for every birth year since 1883, which generations claim it. Its position, set out in the founding statement and restated in every annual report since, is that a generation may be named only prospectively, and that every boundary in current use was drawn after the fact. The Concordance shows 1961 to be the only birth year claimed by three widely used frameworks at once.

Its principal programs are the Cusp Studies Program, directed since 2008 by [Dr. Pauline Esterhaus](/wiki/people/dr-pauline-esterhaus/), which studies the roughly 28.9 million living Americans born between 1960 and 1966; the Working Group on Retroactive Naming; and the Alpha Endpoint Review, opened in 2015 to determine when Generation Alpha ends, which has held forty-four public comment sessions and has not yet reached a recommendation. The Center's 2025 survey of 3,812 cuspal adults found that 61 percent could name their own generation under at least one framework, 23 percent under two, 11.4 percent named a generation to which they could not belong under any framework, and 4.7 percent could not name one at all. The Center designates the last group "unaffiliated."

The Center publishes the quarterly journal *Cohort Studies Review* and occupies the second floor of a former dairy cooperative office on East Johnson Street, in the Tenney–Lapham neighborhood. It has written to three national polling firms requesting that a cuspal respondent's uncertainty no longer be coded as a refusal. Two have acknowledged receipt.

## Private Profile

**Institutional posture:** Scrupulous, prospective and patient to the point of paralysis. The Center believes that the generations are an administrative fiction with real consequences, and it has dedicated itself to documenting the fiction precisely rather than abolishing it. It never tells anyone which generation they are in. It tells them how many claim them, and by what authority.

**Internal tensions:** The Alpha Endpoint Review, now in its twelfth year, has become the Center's most Madisonian undertaking, and several staff members believe it can be concluded only after Generation Alpha has ended, at which point the Center's own founding principle would forbid naming it. The Cusp Studies Program considers itself the Center's moral core and the Concordance its bureaucratic one, and the two are occasionally on speaking terms.

**Adversaries:** The generational marketing industry, which the Center regards as drawing boundaries for sale. National polling firms, which offer cuspal respondents a list and record a question as a refusal.

**Useful for:** Stories about generations, birth cohorts, demographic labels, generational marketing, age-based surveys, and anyone who falls on a line. A natural source on any story in which "which generation are you" is asked and cannot be answered. See [Duane Ziebell](/wiki/people/duane-ziebell/) of [Wausau](/wiki/places/wausau/), whom the Center has classified, at a newspaper's request, as unaffiliated.
