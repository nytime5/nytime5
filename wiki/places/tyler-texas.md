## Public Profile

**Tyler** is the seat of Smith County, in the Piney Woods of East Texas, about a hundred miles east of Dallas. It has called itself the Rose Capital of America since the 1930s, when the surrounding fields produced a large share of the nation's commercial rose bushes, and it maintains the claim with a municipal rose garden of more than thirty thousand bushes and the Texas Rose Festival, first held in 1933, at which a Rose Queen is crowned in a gown whose train is measured, discussed and remembered.

The city is the medical center of East Texas. Health care is its largest employer, and two large hospital systems have built campuses whose towers, parking structures and helipads form a medical district visible from most of the city's main roads. Patients travel to Tyler from across a region of more than a million people for cardiology, oncology and orthopedics. Texas has the highest uninsured rate in the nation and has not expanded Medicaid, and a substantial share of the residents who can see the hospitals from their trucks have not been inside one except through the emergency department.

Tyler's main commercial road is Broadway Avenue, which runs south from the brick-paved downtown square past several decades of strip malls in the order they were built. It is the home of [Remand Health](/wiki/organizations/remand-health/), which opened a storefront on South Broadway in June 2026 for the purpose of being stolen from by uninsured patients who need to be confined in the Smith County jail to receive medical care. The jail, a short drive from the medical district, has since become the most reliable route into it.

## Public Institutions and Fixtures

- **The Tyler Rose Garden** — the municipal rose garden, open to the public and free; every bush is pruned, fed, sprayed and inspected on a documented schedule.
- **The Texas Rose Festival** — October; the Rose Queen, the coronation, the parade.
- **The medical district** — two hospital systems, a medical school, the helipads.
- **The Smith County jail** — budgeted $6.1 million for medical care in fiscal 2026 and exceeded it by $1.4 million by November.
- **The Smith County Commissioners Court** — the county's governing body, which in December 2026 takes up a proposal to fund a public clinic for uninsured residents.
- **The Remand Health storefront** — South Broadway Avenue; the register opens at $812.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** Tyler is the universe's city of care, in two forms: roses, which receive it on a schedule, and hospitals, which provide it to those who qualify. It is a place where the finest medical facilities in a hundred miles stand in plain view of the people who cannot use them, which is why the most efficient health-care company in Texas opened a store on Broadway and waited to be robbed.

**The exaggeration:** Real Tyler is a comfortable regional city with a famous rose garden and a large medical sector. Turn the dial until the two facts face each other: every rose bush in Tyler has a documented care plan and a free public viewing, and every resident of Tyler has a hospital in view and a waiting list. The exaggeration should stay affectionate. Tyler is not callous. It is courteous, churchgoing and practical, and when its people found a way to see a doctor, they took it in an orderly fashion and thanked the clerk.

**Generative fixations:** The rose garden and its maintenance schedule. The Rose Queen's train. The medical district skyline. The waiting list at the county clinic. The Commissioners Court and its December hearing. Broadway Avenue, and what is next door to Remand.
