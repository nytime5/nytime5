## Public Profile

**Menlo Park** is a city in San Mateo County, California, on the San Francisco Peninsula, bordered by Palo Alto to the south and the salt flats of the Bay to the east. It is the home of Sand Hill Road, the two-mile stretch of low buildings and old oaks along which a disproportionate share of the world's venture capital is managed, and of [Fullspeed Capital](/wiki/organizations/fullspeed-capital/), the firm whose general partner described a 10 percent probability of human extinction as the best deal in history. It is also the headquarters of Meta and the site of the SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, neither of which, by local convention, is what the city is known for.

Menlo Park is distinguished among Peninsula cities by a civic temperament organized entirely around the bet. Where its neighbors build the product or review the paperwork, Menlo Park prices the position. Residents evaluate propositions in terms of expected value, refer to disagreements as differences in conviction, and regard any outcome, however severe, as acceptable provided it was modeled. The city council has described the municipal budget as "a portfolio," the planning commission has rejected a proposal as "pre-seed," and the Sharon Heights Safeway, whose parking lot is widely considered the tightest market in town, has a Yelp page on which several reviews discuss allocation.

The city's principal export is capital, and its principal anxiety is being late. The companies Sand Hill Road funds tend to leave for [Sunnyvale](/wiki/places/sunnyvale/) or San Francisco once they have become real, a pattern that residents describe as "the model working" and that gives the city the texture of a place whose successes are always somewhere else. Its housing stock, by contrast, is the one asset in the city that nobody is willing to mark to market, and the city's position on new construction has remained unchanged across every cycle, a consistency residents regard as the only hedge in town.

Menlo Park is prosperous, quiet, and shaded, and its weather is unremarkable. Its residents are, by survey, confident.

## Public Institutions and Fixtures

- **Sand Hill Road** — a corridor of venture firms in buildings designed to look like they are not in a hurry, occupied by people who are.
- **The Sharon Heights Safeway** — a grocery store whose parking lot is the only market in Menlo Park in which demand reliably exceeds supply, and the only one residents describe as a long shot.
- **[Alignment for Kids](/wiki/organizations/alignment-for-kids/)** — an after-school program on Santa Cruz Avenue whose enrollment is treated by some residents as a leading indicator.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** Menlo Park is the universe's capital town, the third point of the Bay Area triangle. [Sunnyvale](/wiki/places/sunnyvale/) supplies the product and the optimization that produces it; [Berkeley](/wiki/places/berkeley-california/) supplies the procedure and the review that documents it; Menlo Park supplies the money and the conviction that the money is a form of judgment. Set a story here when it needs an investor, a term sheet, a quarterly letter, or someone who can state the probability of catastrophe correctly and then explain why it is a buy.

**The exaggeration:** Real Menlo Park is a wealthy, pleasant, somewhat anonymous suburb that happens to contain the most consequential two miles of road in the venture economy. Turn the dial until the road's worldview becomes the city's: a place where everything is a position, every risk is a line item, downside is acceptable as long as it was modeled, and the one unforgivable error is to be late to something. The comedy is that the framework is applied uniformly, including to things that cannot be averaged over.

**Why the satire lands here:** A man who computes the expected value of human extinction and finds it positive is not an anomaly on Sand Hill Road; he is the local register, spoken fluently. The reader should feel that this is where that sentence gets said, in that building, with that view, by someone who has sat on eleven boards.

**Generative fixations:** Conviction as a virtue. Velocity as a moat. A quiet grievance that the companies it funds leave once they matter. The housing position, which is the only thing in town nobody will reprice. The Safeway parking lot, which is a running joke that should stay small.

**Useful contrast:** Against Sunnyvale (nothing is ever wrong) and Berkeley (nothing is ever decided), Menlo Park is the place where everything is decided, immediately, on the numbers, and where the numbers are right.
