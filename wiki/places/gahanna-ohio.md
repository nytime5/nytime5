## Public Profile

**Gahanna** is a city in Franklin County, Ohio, on the northeastern edge of Columbus, bounded on the west by [John Glenn Columbus International Airport](/wiki/places/john-glenn-columbus-international-airport/) and bisected by Big Walnut Creek. It has been the official Herb Capital of Ohio since 1972, by act of the state legislature, a designation it has defended for more than half a century and which no other municipality has contested. It is the home of [Frank Henderson](/wiki/people/frank-henderson/), author of the October 2026 proposal to replace airport screening with a bowl of knives at every jetway.

The city's identity is organized around two facts that its residents regard as unrelated. The first is the herb designation, which is marked by the Ohio Herb Education Center, an annual Herb Day, a municipal herb garden, and a civic conviction that the city's standing in the state rests on its thyme. The second is the airport, whose eastern approach passes overhead at an altitude low enough that long-time residents can distinguish an Airbus from a Boeing by the pitch of the descent, and routinely do so, in conversation, without pausing. Gahanna has lived beneath arriving aircraft for so long that it no longer hears them, in the sense that it hears nothing else.

The arrangement has produced a population with an unusual relationship to air travel. The airport is a six-minute drive from most of the city, and a disproportionate share of its residents fly for a living: sales territories, consulting rotations, the regional management of things. These are people who have been screened more often than most Americans have been to the dentist, who hold strong and specific views on the design of the checkpoint, and who have never once been asked. The city council maintains a standing Airport Liaison Committee whose meetings are well attended and whose recommendations, by its own minutes, have been "received" by the airport authority on forty-three consecutive occasions.

Gahanna's Creekside district, a redevelopment along Big Walnut Creek completed in 2008, is where the city goes to have opinions. Its restaurants are the venue for the kind of practical, confident, irreversible civic conversation in which a man who has just been made to unwrap a sandwich explains, to general agreement, how the whole thing should actually work.

## Public Institutions and Fixtures

- **The Ohio Herb Education Center** — on Mill Street, in the old village; offers classes, sells seedlings, and maintains the city's claim.
- **The Airport Liaison Committee** — convened monthly since 1991; recommendations received, forty-three consecutive times.
- **The approach** — the line in the sky along which the city's aircraft arrive, known locally by flight number rather than airline, and used as a clock.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** Gahanna is the universe's frequent-flyer town — the suburb full of people who know exactly how the system treats them because they are processed by it weekly, and who have, as a consequence, designed better systems in their heads, on napkins, and on the backs of boarding passes. It is where the citizen-engineer lives.

**The exaggeration:** Real Gahanna is a comfortable Columbus suburb beside the airport with a quaint herb designation. Turn the dial until the two facts become the whole civic character: a town that takes thyme as seriously as [Paramus](/wiki/places/paramus/) takes its blue laws, and that has been flown over so often it has become a population of experts with no standing. The exaggeration must stay fond; Gahanna is not aggrieved, it is *practical*, and the practicality is the comedy.

**Why the satire lands here:** Of course the knife bowl came from Gahanna. Nowhere else in the country is a regional flooring salesman screened forty times a year within six minutes of his own kitchen, and nowhere else would the resulting proposal be received, at the Creekside restaurants, as obviously correct.

**Generative fixations:** The herb designation. The approach, and what arrives on it. The committee whose recommendations are received. A citywide, unspoken belief that the people who run the airport have never actually flown.
