## Public Profile

**Elk Grove** is a city in Sacramento County, California, situated approximately fifteen miles south of the state capital along Highway 99. Incorporated in 2000, it was for a period in the mid-2000s the fastest-growing city in the United States, a distinction it acquired quickly, held briefly, and has referred to continuously ever since.

The city's built environment is the product of that expansion: a dense sequence of master-planned subdivisions, arterial roads named for the agricultural operations they replaced, and a downtown district — Old Town Elk Grove — that predates the growth by a century and is regarded by long-tenured residents as the only part of the city that counts. The distinction between Old Town and everything east of it is the central fact of local civic life and is raised, in some form, at nearly every meeting of the City Council.

Elk Grove is best known outside the region as the home of the **Elk Grove Community Chess Club**, an institution founded in 1974 in the back room of a Rotary hall and now operating from a leased storefront in a shopping center on Elk Grove Boulevard. The club's annual invitational is the largest amateur tournament in the Sacramento Valley and has been, since 2011, the subject of a continuing dispute over seeding methodology that has produced four revisions of the club bylaws, two resignations from the tournament committee, and a standing appeal to the state chess association that has never been formally withdrawn. The club has 214 members. Its meetings are minuted.

The city's economy is principally residential and retail. Its largest employers are the school district, the hospital system, and a distribution center. Its annual events calendar includes the Western Festival, the Giant Pumpkin Festival, and the invitational, in ascending order of local seriousness.

## Local Character

**Civic identity:** Elk Grove understands itself as a real place that other people mistake for a suburb of Sacramento, and it resents the mistake in proportion to how often it is made. Residents will specify that Elk Grove is a city, that it has its own council, and that it was incorporated — a sequence delivered as a single unit and rarely solicited.

**Local obsessions:** The seeding methodology at the invitational. The boundary of Old Town. The number of left-turn signals on Elk Grove Boulevard and whether it is the correct number. Whether the city's growth was managed or merely survived. The high school football program, which is good, and the fact that it does not receive the coverage the chess club does, which is a grievance of long standing among a specific set of parents.

**Useful for:** Stories in which an amateur institution has developed the procedural apparatus of a federal agency; disputes over rules that no external authority is obliged to recognize; suburban civic seriousness; and any narrative requiring a community that will convene a committee, take minutes, and produce a written finding on a matter of no consequence to anyone outside it.

**The chess club:** The club is the city's most generative institution and the reason Elk Grove appears in this newspaper more often than a city of its size otherwise would. Its officers hold titles. Its bylaws are amended by recorded vote. Its seeding dispute has outlasted three club presidents and is understood, by everyone involved, to be about something other than seeding.
