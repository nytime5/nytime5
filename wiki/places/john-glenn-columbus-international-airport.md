## Public Profile

**John Glenn Columbus International Airport** is the principal commercial airport serving central Ohio, located six miles east of downtown Columbus and immediately west of [Gahanna](/wiki/places/gahanna-ohio/). It was renamed in 2016 for the astronaut and senator, who was present for the ceremony. It handles roughly nine million passengers a year through three concourses, lettered A, B and C, and describes itself in its own materials as "a gateway," without specifying to what.

The airport's distinguishing characteristic is a checkpoint culture of unusual thoroughness. With no transatlantic service and no hub carrier, its screening operation has long had more capacity than demand, and the resulting attention is applied, in full, to whoever is in the lane. Transportation security officers at Columbus are regarded within the agency as exceptionally diligent and by the traveling public as exceptionally present. Secondary inspections are frequent, unhurried, and conducted with a courtesy that regular passengers describe as the most unsettling part. Items flagged at Columbus include, in the public record, a sandwich whose mayonnaise was the subject of an eleven-minute discussion in October 2026, during which the passenger, [Frank Henderson](/wiki/people/frank-henderson/) of Gahanna, maintained that the condiment was "obviously not a liquid," and the officer maintained the agency's published guidance, which is contextual.

It was at Gate B22 of Concourse B that Mr. Henderson, on October 13, 2026, described to *The New York Time5* his proposal to replace checkpoint screening with a bowl of knives at every jetway, and it was at the Concourse B checkpoint, earlier that morning, that the boarding pass on which he had drawn the proposal passed through the X-ray in a bin, face up, and was imaged. The agency has confirmed that the bins are imaged. It has characterized its assessment of the sketch as ongoing.

The airport authority has for several years pursued a nonstop route to Europe, an effort it describes as "advanced" and which has produced a succession of announcements, each received in Gahanna with the practiced calm of a population that has already heard the aircraft.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** The universe's house airport for domestic air-travel stories — the checkpoint where the system works exactly as designed on a passenger who has thoughts about it. It is not a bad airport. It is a thorough one, with time on its hands, which is the more dangerous condition for a traveler with a sandwich.

**The exaggeration:** Real Columbus is a clean, mid-sized, well-run airport with a long-standing ambition for European service. Turn the dial until thoroughness becomes the local art form: a screening operation so attentive that nothing passes without being seen, imaged, retained and assessed, and a checkpoint at which the public and the agency meet as equals in the conviction that the other has never flown.

**Why the satire lands here:** The knife bowl needed a checkpoint good enough to inspire it and attentive enough to intercept it, and Columbus is both. The system Mr. Henderson proposes to abolish is the one that read his proposal first.

**Generative fixations:** The imaged bins. The European route. The gate screening. The published guidance, which is contextual. Concourse B.
