## Overview

**Austin** is the capital of Texas and the seat of Travis County, on the Colorado River where it leaves the Hill Country for the coastal plain. It is home to the State Capitol, the flagship campus of the University of Texas, and an annual music, film and technology festival that residents describe as the reason they moved there and also the reason they are thinking of leaving.

The city has been among the fastest-growing large metropolitan areas in the United States for most of the past three decades. Federal estimates of internal migration routinely cite it by name, and a 2026 Congressional Budget Office projection observed that the roughly 23 million Americans who changed address in 2024 had moved, "mostly to Austin." Its economy rests on state government, the university and a technology sector that expanded sharply after 2020, when several large companies, among them Tesla, relocated headquarters or major operations to the area.

The east side of the city, including the East Cesar Chavez neighborhood between Interstate 35 and the river, is among the most closely watched districts in Texas for changes in who lives where, and residents of every tenure discuss those changes at length, in the same coffee shops.

## Local Character

Austin's official civic identity is that it is weird, a claim made on bumper stickers, T-shirts and municipal-adjacent signage since the early 2000s, and defended with the earnestness ordinarily reserved for a state religion. The weirdness is understood to be under permanent threat. Austin has been declared "over" by its own residents in every year since at least 1983, each time by someone who arrived in the year before the declaration and regards themselves, accordingly, as having been there when it was good.

Seniority is measured in years since arrival and is very short. A resident of four years is an old-timer and speaks of the newcomers who ruined it. A resident of two years has opinions on the newcomers who ruined it. A resident of six months knows the correct breakfast taco and will tell you. Natives exist but are rarely asked.

The city's newer industries have given it a second identity as a place where the future is beta-tested on the people who moved there to see it. Austin residents are early adopters by migration as much as by temperament, and the city is unusually rich in people who have reorganized their working lives around a new tool and are still working out the terms.

## Private Profile

**What this place is for:** Austin is the universe's test market for the individual adopter: the place where technology stories become household stories, set at a kitchen table rather than a laboratory. Where [Menlo Park](/wiki/places/menlo-park/) supplies the capital and [Berkeley](/wiki/places/berkeley-california/) the laboratory, Austin supplies the user, newly arrived, fully committed, and describing the experience in the first person.

**The comedy:** A city that defines itself by resistance to change and has changed faster than almost any other American city, and whose residents compete to have arrived slightly earlier than one another. Every Austin source is either a recent convert to something or an aggrieved veteran of four years.

**Avoid:** Mocking the neighborhoods' longtime residents or the city's Mexican-American history. The joke is the newcomer's self-regard, never the people who were there first.
