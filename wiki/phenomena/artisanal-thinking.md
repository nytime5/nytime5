## Public Profile

**Artisanal thinking**, also called **hand-reasoning**, is the practice of arriving at a decision through reasoning performed entirely by one person, in real time, without reference to any device, guideline, checklist, decision-support system or colleague. Practitioners distinguish it from ordinary unaided thought by its deliberateness: the artisanal thinker does not merely lack assistance but has declined it, usually aloud, and regards the declination as part of the work.

The practice emerged in the East Bay of California in about 2024 among professionals whose fields had adopted automated decision support, and is most closely associated with [Dr. Everett Lindqvist](/wiki/people/dr-everett-lindqvist/), a general surgeon at [Codornices Medical Center](/wiki/organizations/codornices-medical-center/) in [Berkeley](/wiki/places/berkeley-california/), who on Oct. 13, 2026, dismissed a robotic surgical system from a gallbladder removal in progress and completed the procedure by narrated reasoning. Dr. Lindqvist is one of at least four surgeons in the region who identify with the practice. It has no formal organization. It has a newsletter, *The Unassisted*, published irregularly, and, since September 2026, a tote bag reading "THOUGHT BY HAND."

Practitioners describe the practice in the vocabulary of craft provenance. A decision may be "single-origin" (reached by one person), "small-batch" (reached for one case, with no intention of generalizing) or "encountered" (reached in the presence of the thing decided about). The industrialization of bread, coffee and furniture is cited as precedent; the claim is that cognition has undergone the same process, with the same gains in consistency and the same unnamed loss. The term "slow thinking" is rejected by practitioners as a pejorative coined by finance offices.

The [Institute for Occupational Cognitive Health](/wiki/organizations/institute-for-occupational-cognitive-health/) in Baltimore has tracked what it calls "unassisted reasoning events" in operating rooms since 2022, and reports that the count has grown every year. Its 2025 paper on the subject found that surgeons who reasoned without external support for more than forty consecutive minutes exhibited a 31 percent increase in audible self-narration and a measurable decline in the willingness to be handed things. The institute's director, [Dr. Franklin Krause](/wiki/people/dr-franklin-krause/), has said that the institute does not recommend the practice but recognizes it.

Critics, principally hospital administrators and the manufacturers of decision-support systems, note that the practice extends procedure times substantially; that the "unassisted" reasoner brings to the task decades of training that were, at the time, other people's thinking; and that the checklist was introduced for reasons that have not changed. Practitioners generally concede all three points and regard none of them as responsive.

The practice has a precedent in theoretical physics, where working alone at a desk without reference to results one has not obtained oneself was the field's ordinary method until about 1930. [Dr. Rolf Ekelund](/wiki/people/dr-rolf-ekelund/), a professor at the California Institute of Technology, has observed the practice's constraints in full since 2019 and has concluded that every problem in physics that satisfies them has already been solved. He is the movement's elegist, as Dr. Lindqvist is its exponent.

## Private Profile

**What this is for:** The universe's name for the deliberate refusal of cognitive automation as a craft identity. It is a movement in the way sourdough is a movement: a small number of sincere practitioners, a vocabulary borrowed from provenance, a tote bag, and a large number of people who find the whole thing both admirable and much too long. It can be applied to any profession that has adopted decision support: surgery, aviation, law, accounting, air traffic control, grading, parenting.

**The comedy:** The provenance language applied to thought, delivered with total sincerity, and the time cost, borne by everyone else. The practice is never wrong. Dr. Lindqvist's patient was fine. The decision the artisanal thinker reaches after nine minutes is the decision the machine displayed in the first second. The loss the movement cannot name is real, and so is the clock.

**Avoid:** Making practitioners incompetent or their outcomes bad. The artisanal thinker is excellent at the task; that is what makes the refusal legible as a choice rather than a failure. Also avoid letting the movement acquire an institute. Its lack of organization is its character; the moment it has a director, it has become the thing it opposes, and that is a different story, to be written later.
