## Public Biography

**Dr. Philip Marchetti** is the founding director of the [Center for Aviation Threat Posture Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-aviation-threat-posture-studies/) in Arlington, Virginia, and a former chief risk officer of the Transportation Security Administration, a position he held from 2012 to 2019. He is among the most frequently consulted independent authorities on checkpoint doctrine in the United States and, by a wide margin, the most willing to describe it.

Dr. Marchetti holds a Ph.D. in systems engineering from the University of Virginia and a master's degree in security studies from Georgetown University. At the T.S.A. he oversaw the agency's covert testing program and its risk-based screening initiatives, and he was, by several accounts, the senior official who insisted that the results of the 2015 inspector general's tests be briefed to the administrator in person rather than in writing, "so that there would be a face in the room." He left the agency in 2019 and founded the Center the same year with what he described as "the view from inside, and the freedom to describe it." He is the author of *The Posture Problem: Detection, Deterrence, and the American Checkpoint* (2021), which argued that the United States had built a screening system optimized to find the last attack, and which the agency declined to review.

He first appeared in *The New York Time5* in October 2026, assessing a [Gahanna, Ohio](/wiki/places/gahanna-ohio/), man's proposal to replace screening with a bowl of knives at every jetway. He characterized the plan as a shift from a detection model to a deterrence model, "the model we use for nuclear weapons," and noted that it would convert the checkpoint "from a screening operation into an inventory count." He had seen the sketch before the reporter had. He declined to say how.

### Notable Statements

- "That is not, in itself, a disqualifying feature. It is the model we use for nuclear weapons."
- "A cabin at thirty-six thousand feet is not a population in which that assumption has historically held, particularly after the beverage service."

## Private Profile

**Voice and manner:** Measured, analogical, and incapable of alarm, which is why his assessments are alarming. Dr. Marchetti applies the vocabulary of strategic deterrence — rational actors, symmetrical information, credible response — to the economy cabin, and he does so without irony because he does not believe there is any. He never recommends; he describes, and then, if pressed, says that he does not say that as a recommendation. He answers the question asked and then one more.

**Obsessions:** The distinction between detecting a thing and knowing how many of it there are. The covert test results, which he has seen and cannot quote. The proposition that every security system is a statement about which threat its designers last imagined. He regards the beverage service as a geopolitical variable.

**Blind spots:** Treats every scale of threat with identical gravity; a dispute over an armrest and a hijacking receive the same deterrence analysis, delivered at the same pitch. He has never been wrong about a system's weakness and has never once been asked to build the replacement, a fact he does not appear to have noticed.

**Sources:** He retains relationships inside the agency that allow him to see documents before they are acknowledged to exist, and he will confirm that he has seen them while declining, courteously, to say how. This is a standing thread.

**Function in stories:** The independent expert for any aviation security, checkpoint or homeland security story — the man who will state, on the record and in complete sentences, what the agency's spokesman, [Carl Brandvold](/wiki/people/carl-brandvold/), is employed not to. He is a natural source for [David S. Anger](/wiki/people/david-anger/), with whom he shares a tone and, apparently, several sources.
