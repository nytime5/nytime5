## Public Biography

**Dr. Terrence Kolbeck** is a psycholinguist and the founding director of the [Center for Discursive Throughput](/wiki/organizations/center-for-discursive-throughput/) at the University of Michigan, where he has held a joint appointment in psychology and communication since 2014. He was the principal investigator of *Throughput Limits in the Oral Expression of Political Belief*, the four-year federally funded study transmitted to the Senate Committee on Rules and Administration in August 2026.

Dr. Kolbeck holds a Ph.D. in cognitive psychology from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where his dissertation concerned the interval between the formation of an intention to speak and the production of the first word — a gap he measured at a mean of 640 milliseconds and described, in the dissertation's final chapter, as "the most consequential unexamined moment in human affairs." He spent nine years at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics in Nijmegen before returning to the United States.

His early work concerned ordinary speech disfluency. It was redirected in 2011, he has said, by an experience he has declined to describe in interviews and has described twice in print: an argument with his brother-in-law, conducted over roughly four minutes at a family gathering, after which his brother-in-law repeated back to a third party a position that Dr. Kolbeck had not held and had, in the four minutes, been attempting to disown. He wrote the founding prospectus of the center within the year.

He is the author of *The Ordering Problem* (MIT Press, 2019) and a co-author of *Fidelity Loss in Spoken Belief* (2023). He does not accept invitations to appear on live radio.

Dr. Kolbeck lives in Ann Arbor.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Dr. Kolbeck speaks the way a man speaks when he has proven, to his own satisfaction and with federal money, that speaking does not work. He is not defeated by this. He is a careful empiricist who has arrived at an unusually bleak result and reports it in the same tone he would use for any other result, which is what makes him quotable. He is generous with his time and terrible at using it, because he cannot stop qualifying.

**Verbal tics:** Restarts sentences. Will say a thing, stop, and say "no — let me put that a different way," sometimes three or four times, and each successive version is better than the last, which he knows, which is why he keeps going. Uses "on our data" the way other people use "I think." Refers to a person's total political position as "the position" and to any spoken version of it as "an utterance," a distinction he maintains rigorously and which listeners rarely notice.

**Obsessions:** The first clause. The 640 milliseconds. The fact that the order of a spoken argument is invented at the doorway and then determines everything downstream. The question of whether writing is genuinely a parallel medium or merely a slower serial one — he suspects the latter and has not been able to show it. What his brother-in-law told the third party.

**Blind spots:** Cannot see that his own habit of restarting sentences is the single most vivid instance of his findings available to any reporter in the room, and that it is why his quotes are good. When this has been pointed out, he has asked for the recording.

**Tone:** Precise, self-interrupting, unbitter. He has the equanimity of a man who has stopped expecting to be understood and has redirected the resulting energy into measurement.

**Function in stories:** Kolbeck is the standing expert for any story about the gap between what a person means and what a person conveys — political silence, misquotation, the collapse of a nuanced position into a slogan, the failure of an explanation. He does not moralize about discourse. He treats it as a throughput problem with a fixed ceiling, and he will give the number.
