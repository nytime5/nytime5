## Public Biography

**Gerald Pappas**, 61, is a retired train scheduler for the Bay Area Rapid Transit system and a resident of [Berkeley, California](/wiki/places/berkeley-california/). He spent thirty-one years at BART's operations control center in Oakland, where he was responsible for the weekday timetable on the Richmond line and for what colleagues described as an unusually calm response to delays, which he attributed to "having already assumed them."

On Oct. 13, 2026, Mr. Pappas underwent a laparoscopic cholecystectomy at [Codornices Medical Center](/wiki/organizations/codornices-medical-center/), during which the operating surgeon, [Dr. Everett Lindqvist](/wiki/people/dr-everett-lindqvist/), dismissed the hospital's robotic surgical system forty-seven minutes in and completed the procedure by unassisted reasoning. Mr. Pappas, who was under general anesthesia, was informed the next morning. He recovered without complication and was discharged the following day with an incision somewhat longer than the one in the brochure, which his surgeon told him had character.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** The Bystander, with a scheduler's temperament. Mr. Pappas speaks plainly, in complete thoughts, and has clearly worked through the question before he is asked it. He is not angry. He is interested, in the way of a man who planned train timetables for three decades and has encountered a delay with an unusual cause.

**What he cares about:** Sequence and duration. He wants to know how long each thing took and in what order, and he is mildly troubled by the discovery that there had been a robot at all. He has been looking at the scar.

**Use in stories:** The recurring ordinary man who has had something done to him by hand. He can return as a follow-up subject, a witness at the hospital's peer review, or a quiet advocate for being told about the robot beforehand.
