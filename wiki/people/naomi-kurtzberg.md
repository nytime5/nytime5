## Public Biography

**Naomi Kurtzberg** has been, since 2002, the general counsel of the [National Council of the Easily Annoyed](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/) in Washington, the governing body of the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/). She drafted Article 9 of the council's charter, which since 2004 has permitted a member to except himself from a provision by reservation, and is the author of the memorandum of Oct. 9, 2026, that found admissible the first reservation against Article 1: a member's objection to the word "community."

Ms. Kurtzberg, 63, graduated from Barnard College in 1985 and from the Georgetown University Law Center in 1988. She spent the next fourteen years in the Office of the Legal Adviser at the State Department, the last twelve of them in its treaty affairs office, where she worked on *Treaties in Force*, the department's annual list of the treaties and other international agreements in force for the United States, and on the depositary functions the department performs for multilateral treaties deposited with the United States, which include receiving one government's reservations, circulating them to the other governments concerned and receiving those governments' objections.

She joined the council's staff in 2002. In 2004 she drafted Article 9, which the council adopted that year, on the model of the articles on reservations in the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, which the United States signed in 1970 and has never ratified. Its test, that a reservation be "not incompatible with the object and purpose of the Council," is taken from the convention nearly word for word. Her draft shortened the twelve months the convention allows for objection to ninety days and provided that an objection does not undo a reservation but is recorded beside it permanently. "A member who cannot accept one provision of the charter has at present one remedy, which is to leave," she wrote in the explanatory note that accompanied the draft. "A charter should not offer its members only the door."

Article 9 also names the general counsel as the depositary of reservations and of the objections to them and, following the convention's requirement that a depositary act impartially, provides that the general counsel may not be a member of the council. Ms. Kurtzberg has never been a member. She has not completed the council's 140-item eligibility questionnaire, on which an answer of "very" or "extremely" to any three items is sufficient for membership, and has declined, when asked, to say how she would answer it.

Members entered 4,118 reservations between the adoption of Article 9 and September 2026, most of them to the dues schedule and the remainder chiefly to the typeface of the newsletter, and Ms. Kurtzberg has issued a written determination on each; most run to a single page. Her determination of 2004 on reservations to the dues schedule found them admissible and held that they do not discharge the dues, and most reservations to the schedule have since arrived enclosed with payment. A determination of 2011 found reservations to the typeface of the newsletter admissible and held that, the newsletter being printed in a single edition, they are given effect by being recorded.

[Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/), a retired title examiner in [Takoma Park](/wiki/places/takoma-park/), Md., and a member since 1997, wrote to the council in a four-page letter dated Sept. 14, 2026. The letter objects to the word "community" where it appears in Article 1, which describes the membership as "the easily annoyed community," and asks that the word not be applied to him "in any correspondence, roster, newsletter, census or remark," or, in the alternative, that his membership be terminated. The council treated it as a reservation under Article 9. None of the earlier reservations had been entered against Article 1. In an eleven-page memorandum dated Oct. 9, Ms. Kurtzberg found the reservation admissible. The council's object and purpose under Article 2 is "to represent persons who are easily annoyed, and to see that their annoyance is recorded," and a member annoyed by the charter itself was, she wrote, not incompatible with that purpose but "squarely within it." The memorandum further noted that the membership office scores all incoming correspondence against the questionnaire's five-point scale, that it had scored each page of Mr. Ebersole's letter "extremely," and that the letter therefore met the standard for membership on its own. On that basis, the council renewed his membership through Dec. 31, 2028. The alternative request was not reached.

"Mr. Ebersole wrote that a community has no boundary, and that he therefore could not know whether he was in one," Ms. Kurtzberg said by telephone. "This one has a boundary. It is the top two categories of a five-point scale, and his letter is in the top category on every page. I could not have declined to renew him consistent with the charter. I would add that I did not want to." Mr. Ebersole's new card, which arrived on Oct. 20, describes him, in accordance with the reservation, as "Member, Easily Annoyed." The reservation was circulated to the membership on Oct. 12, and by Oct. 30 the council had received 1,114 objections, more than had been filed against all 4,118 previous reservations combined. The period closes on Jan. 10, 2027. At Mr. Ebersole's request, her office is sending him a copy of each objection; the first two hundred arrived on Oct. 31. [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/), a senior fellow at the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/), said the memorandum was the first answer she had seen in writing, from a body with the authority to give one, to the question of whether an explicitly undertaken refusal to participate constitutes a participation.

Ms. Kurtzberg lives in the Cleveland Park neighborhood of Washington with her husband, a retired court reporter. On the wall of her office, framed, is the original of the first reservation entered under Article 9, which concerned the dues schedule and is one sentence long.

## Private Profile

**Demeanor:** Exact, unhurried and uncontracted. Ms. Kurtzberg speaks the way she drafts, in holdings rather than opinions, and states procedure as fact: the reservation is circulated, the period is ninety days, the alternative request was not reached. She does not raise her voice or use an adjective she could not define. When she offers her own preference she offers it last, in a separate sentence, after the holding has been stated in full.

**Core conviction:** That a complaint is not a malfunction of a membership organization but its substance. Her maxim, which she gives to new staff of the membership office and has never said for publication, is: "Most organizations treat a complaint as a problem to be resolved. We treat it as the relationship." She regards Article 9 as the provision that made the second sentence true. Before 2004 a complaint about the charter could end only in a resignation, which in her view is a relationship that ended for want of procedure.

**What she admires:** A well-made complaint. She reads every reservation and every objection in full, enclosures included, and intends to have read every objection to the reservation of [Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/) before the period closes. She regards his letter as the best-drafted correspondence the [National Council of the Easily Annoyed](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/) has received since 2004, and has said so to colleagues, citing its enumeration: "correspondence, roster, newsletter, census or remark."

**What she does not say:** Anything about a member's temperament. She applies the five-point scale to correspondence and never to people: a letter is scored "extremely"; its author is a member. Asked to describe a member, she describes what he wrote, when it was received and how it was scored, and regards the description as complete.

**Verbal habits:** "Consistent with the charter." "On that basis." "Was not reached." "I would add." Cites provisions by number, dates by day and month, and documents by page count. Refers to the membership office's scores as findings.

**Blind spots:** The distinction Mr. Ebersole draws between being counted and being gathered is not one she recognizes. To her, a boundary is all that a community requires, and a five-point scale supplies one. She does not regard a procedure without an exit as defective. The view of [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/) that, on the data currently available, there is no door by which Mr. Ebersole could leave that the council would not record as an entrance is, to Ms. Kurtzberg, an accurate summary of Article 9 rather than a criticism of it.

**Useful for:** Any story in which someone tries to leave an organization, or to remain in one on his own terms; charters, bylaws, reservations, resignations and standing objections; the law of treaties applied to institutions that are not states. She pairs with Dr. Murch, who studies nonparticipation and treats the Oct. 9 memorandum as a primary source, and with [Evelyn Garvey](/wiki/people/evelyn-garvey/), who chairs the council's Montgomery County chapter and holds that the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/) is the easiest thing in the world to run, a view Ms. Kurtzberg shares and could support with citations.

**Open questions:** The period for objections closes on Jan. 10, 2027, after which every objection remains recorded beside the reservation permanently. Article 9 makes no provision for a reply to an objection; she is aware of the omission. She has a view on what the council would do with a letter of resignation scored "extremely," and has not been asked for it.
