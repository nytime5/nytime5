## Public Biography

**Dr. Thaddeus Krill** is the founder and director of the [Center for Post-Grid Exchange](/wiki/organizations/center-for-post-grid-exchange/) in Bozeman, Montana, and the leading authority on how prices would form in the United States after the collapse of the electrical grid.

Dr. Krill, 58, holds a Ph.D. in economics from the University of Chicago, where his dissertation examined the cigarette economy of Stalag VII-A. He taught at Montana State University for eleven years, during which he published widely on informal markets, including a monograph on the mackerel-pouch economy of federal prisons that remains, by his account, the only economics book ever ordered in bulk by the Bureau of Prisons. He left the university in 2017 to found the Center.

In November 2026 he offered qualified support to [Glenn Torrance](/wiki/people/glenn-torrance/), a Naperville, Ill., software developer who had stockpiled more than 11,000 tablets of erectile dysfunction medication as a currency for use after a Black Sky event. Dr. Krill agreed that the medication met every requirement of commodity money and carried an unusual premium for discretion, but warned that counterfeits would flood the market and that the settlement period was roughly one hour.

He does not himself keep a stockpile, which he has described as a conflict of interest.

## Private Profile

**Voice and manner:** Dry, patient, faintly professorial. Dr. Krill speaks about the end of the world exactly as he would speak about soybean futures, and has never once raised his voice about either. He is fond of the phrases "the spread," "settlement" and "the market has already priced this in," the last of which he uses about markets that do not yet exist. He never says whether a collapse will happen. He finds the question unprofessional.

**The core mechanic:** Every one of his observations is grounded in real economic history, and every one is applied to a world that has not occurred yet. He is, in effect, the beat economist for the apocalypse, and he would like to be quoted accurately.

**Useful for:** Any story involving scarcity, preparedness, barter, outages or hoarding. A natural source for [Amara Okafor-Williams](/wiki/people/amara-okafor-williams/), who regards him as one of the few people she interviews who keeps up.
