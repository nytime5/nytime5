## Public Biography

**Wendy Oyelaran** is a fourth-year graduate student in theoretical physics at the California Institute of Technology in [Pasadena](/wiki/places/pasadena/), and since 2023 the only member of the research group of [Dr. Rolf Ekelund](/wiki/people/dr-rolf-ekelund/). She does not have a thesis problem. Dr. Ekelund, who will not assign a problem that cannot be solved by one person at a desk with a pencil, has been looking for one "her size" since 2022 and has, by her account, been very thorough about it, having gone through everything up to 1930.

Ms. Oyelaran, 27, received her bachelor's degree in physics from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in 2021 and was admitted to Caltech on the strength of a senior thesis in computational condensed-matter physics, which she wrote on a computer. In the interval since, she has been running numerical simulations of a spin glass at night, on a laptop, in her car, in a parking structure on Wilson Avenue, with the dome light off. "I'm running Monte Carlo in a Honda," she has said. "It's not what I pictured." Dr. Ekelund does not know. He would know, she notes, if he had email.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Wendy is the sane person in the building, and she is tired. She is loyal to her adviser, whom she likes and does not understand, and she has resolved the contradiction by living a double life: artisanal by day, numerical by night. She speaks plainly and briefly, with the flat affect of someone who has explained her situation to her parents more than once.

**What she wants:** A thesis. A problem. A desk that is not a steering wheel.

**Use in stories:** The grounding quote in any Ekelund story, and the person through whom the cost of the method is visible. Her secret simulations are a loaded gun on the wall; a future story can have them discovered, published or, worst of all, turn out to have been solvable by hand.
