## Public Biography

**Evelyn Garvey**, 71, has chaired the Montgomery County chapter of the [National Council of the Easily Annoyed](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/) since 2012. She has been a member of the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/) since 1988 and has lived since 1986 on Tulip Avenue in [Takoma Park, Maryland](/wiki/places/takoma-park/), four doors from [Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/), whom she has known since he bought his bungalow there in 1990 and whom she calls Ken.

Ms. Garvey holds a bachelor's degree in sociology from the University of Maryland. From 1983 until her retirement in 2012, the year she became chair, she moderated consumer focus groups for a market-research firm in Rockville, where by her own records she led 3,117 sessions, on subjects that included frozen waffles, long-distance calling plans and the wording of a county recycling flyer. Each began, as is the practice in the field, with fifteen minutes of introductions.

An answer of "very" or "extremely" on any three items of the council's 140-item eligibility questionnaire is sufficient for membership. Ms. Garvey's file lists nine, among them automated telephone menus that announce that their options have recently changed, adhesive name tags, the question "Are you still working on that?" and people who describe themselves as easily annoyed without having completed the questionnaire.

The chapter meets on the second Tuesday of each month in the Lilac Room of the municipal building at 7500 Maple Avenue, which Ms. Garvey calls the community center and Mr. Ebersole calls the Sam Abbott Citizens' Center. The parties have yet to reach consensus. Meetings under Ms. Garvey begin at 7 p.m. and last an average of thirty-eight minutes. There are no introductions and no name tags. The agenda has a single item, "Annoyances," under which members state, without being called on, what has annoyed them since the previous meeting; Ms. Garvey enters each in the minutes in the member's own words, and no member may respond to another's. Motions are not put to a vote. Where members disagree on chapter business, the disagreement is entered beside the matter in dispute, in the manner of an objection under Article 9 of the council's charter, and the meeting moves on. She closes every meeting with the date of the next one and the words "That's all."

New members are not welcomed, a practice the chapter adopted in 2013 at the request of new members. Each December Ms. Garvey sends every member a card, unsigned, giving the date of the January meeting and nothing else. Since 2014 she has declined five requests from market-research firms to recruit participants at chapter meetings.

In November 2026, *The New York Time5* quoted Ms. Garvey in its [report on Mr. Ebersole's objection](/2026-11-02/member-easily-annoyed-community-annoyed-by-word-community/) to the word "community" in Article 1 of the council's charter, which the council had accepted as a reservation and treated as grounds to renew his membership through 2028. "People assume a community like ours must be unpleasant to run. It is the easiest thing in the world," she said. "Nobody waits to be asked what's bothering them, so there's no small talk, and nobody expects to agree. Ken being annoyed by the word is perfectly normal." Of the council's 2019 review of the word, in which a working group found each of fourteen possible replacements more annoying than the original, she said: "Without the word, we're twenty-three people in the Lilac Room who are annoyed separately."

She has kept the minutes of every meeting she has chaired. They are filed in binders in her dining room and record 11,206 annoyances and no resolutions.

## Private Profile

**Demeanor:** Brisk, matter-of-fact and never hurried, because she started on time. Ms. Garvey treats annoyance as an ordinary condition, like the pollen count: it is reported, recorded and not discussed. She is neither a scold nor a sufferer. She does not complain; she registers. She has no small talk and has not missed it.

**What she cares about:** That annoyance is recorded, which she regards as the whole purpose of the [National Council of the Easily Annoyed](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/) and most of its dignity. The second Tuesday. Seven o'clock. The minutes, which she writes herself and never summarizes. The edge of the category: "easily annoyed" belongs to people who have taken the questionnaire, and she hears it from anyone else as one of her nine.

**Verbal habits:** Calls "perfectly normal" anything other people would remark on. Opens a telephone call with the reason for calling and closes it, as she closes a meeting, with "That's all." Keeps the moderator's habit of restating what someone has said in fewer words and moving on: "The leaf blower. Noted." Uses a first name only once she has known someone ten years. Says "community" plainly, as a filing category, with neither warmth nor irony.

**On Ken:** She is fond of [Kenneth Ebersole](/wiki/people/kenneth-ebersole/) and would not put it that way; the fondness shows only as logistics. She is privately annoyed that Ken is annoyed by the word, and regards this as perfectly normal; it is the condition the chapter exists to record. She enters her own annoyances in the minutes like anyone else's. This one she has not entered, and she has not said why. Whether she is among the members who have filed objections to his reservation is not on the record, and it should stay off the record until a story needs it.

**Avoid:** Softening her into a den mother or hardening her into a crank. She does not mediate between Ken and the council, takes no side and does not regard the matter as a dispute. She never says that she is fond of anyone. She never explains why the chapter works; she says it is easy.

**Useful for:** The local voice in any story about the council or the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/), and the resident to call in any [Takoma Park](/wiki/places/takoma-park/) story that needs someone who has already registered an opinion. She is the chapter-level counterpart to the council's general counsel, [Naomi Kurtzberg](/wiki/people/naomi-kurtzberg/): Ms. Kurtzberg finds the community's boundary in the top two categories of a five-point scale, Ms. Garvey in the Lilac Room on the second Tuesday. The chapter's meetings of Nov. 10 and Dec. 8 fall inside the objection period on Mr. Ebersole's reservation, which closes Jan. 10, and the minutes of both will be complete. The minutes she has kept since 2012 are the kind of archive that [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/) of the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/) would ask to see. Ms. Garvey would ask what for.
