## Public Biography

**Wade Castleberry** is the founder and chief executive of [Remand Health](/wiki/organizations/remand-health/), the [Tyler, Texas](/wiki/places/tyler-texas/), company whose only service is being stolen from by uninsured patients who need to be in jail to receive medical care.

Mr. Castleberry, 44, was born and raised in Tyler and holds a degree in health-care administration from the University of Texas at Tyler. He spent fourteen years as a prior-authorization manager at a large health insurer, reviewing requests for treatment against the plan's coverage criteria. He estimates that he denied or delayed some 40,000 of them. He has described the job as "the most thorough education in American health care a person can get without being a patient," and he left it in 2024.

He has said that the idea for Remand came to him during his last year at the insurer, when he noticed that the denial-letter template listed every avenue of appeal available to a patient except the one guaranteed by the Constitution. He spent eighteen months researching Texas theft thresholds, sentencing patterns in Smith County and the case law that followed *Estelle v. Gamble* before opening the company's storefront on South Broadway Avenue in June 2026. The company raised a $14 million Series A in November 2026, led by [Fullspeed Capital](/wiki/organizations/fullspeed-capital/).

He works most days from a stool behind the store's counter, positioned out of the line of sight of the register. He wears a navy quarter-zip pullover.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Wade Castleberry talks like a benefits administrator because he was one, and he has carried the vocabulary out of the insurance industry intact: eligibility, coverage, formulary, qualifying event, loss of coverage. He is mild, patient and East Texas polite, and he does not raise his voice about anything. He is not bitter about his old job. He regards it as research.

**The core mechanic:** He knows exactly where the line is drawn in American health care because he spent fourteen years drawing it, one letter at a time. Remand is what happens when the person who enforced the eligibility rules starts helping people meet them. He has no theory of reform and does not want one. He thinks the system is perfectly legible, and that his contribution is reading it aloud.

**Verbal tics:** Says "eligible" where others would say "sick." Refers to clients' sentences as "coverage periods" and to probation as a loss of coverage. Describes his former employer only as "the plan." Never uses the words "crime," "steal" or "rob" about his own clients; they "qualify."

**Blind spots:** He does not see that he could simply be advocating for the county clinic, because a clinic is not a business, and he has never worked anywhere that was not one. He considers the man who buys water and ibuprofen and leaves to be outside his scope, which in a narrow sense is true.

**Avoid:** Making him a cynic or a con man. He is sincere, his arithmetic is correct and his clients get their surgeries. He is the most effective health-care provider in Smith County, and the only one with a waiting list that moves.
