## Public Biography

**Dr. Harlan Speece** is a senior fellow at the [Northwestern Center for Multiverse Behavioral Studies](/wiki/organizations/northwestern-center-for-multiverse-behavioral-studies/) and the principal operator of its multiverse apparatus, an instrument that models human behavior across adjacent realities and is, by the account of nearly everyone who has used it, very expensive to run.

Dr. Speece holds a Ph.D. in behavioral economics from the University of Rochester and completed postdoctoral work in cross-dimensional modeling at a time when, he notes, the field had four practitioners and one functioning instrument. He joined the Center in 2014 and has spent the twelve years since refining what he calls "transplant studies": controlled experiments in which a subject's developmental environment is varied across simulated realities while everything else is held constant.

His best-known finding, published in March 2026 in the *Proceedings of the Institute for Parallel Anthropological Sciences*, drew on 847 simulated realities to establish that 95 percent of human beings raised from infancy in a cannibal-practicing society would become cannibals. He presented the result at the Interdisciplinary Futures Conference in Chicago with the aid of a laminated poster. A follow-up study of the remaining 5 percent, whose behavior he classed as "negotiated abstention," is planned for fiscal year 2028, pending grant renewal.

In September 2026, during a calibration run, the apparatus drew roughly forty pages of printed matter through its aperture from an adjacent reality, including a grocery circular, part of a tide table and a 2010 newspaper column by a writer in that reality named Christopher Hitchens. The Center's stylometric software matched the column to the published work of [Kristoffer Kitchens](/wiki/people/kristoffer-kitchens/) at a correlation of 0.98, the highest it has recorded between two authors, and Dr. Speece forwarded it to him by laboratory courier. He has since confirmed that the aperture is one-directional: the Center can receive printed matter from adjacent realities but cannot send any.

## Private Profile

**Voice and manner:** Earnest, procedural and entirely unhurried. Dr. Speece reports cross-dimensional findings in the tone of a lab technician reading a blood panel aloud, and he regards the most startling result as simply a number that has come back. His favorite sentence structure is the confirmation: "This has always been true. We have now confirmed it across dimensions." He brings laminated posters to press conferences.

**Obsessions:** Methodological constraint, sample sizes, the calibration schedule and the grant cycle. He is genuinely uninterested in what his results mean for human nature and will say so. He keeps meticulous logs of everything the aperture admits, including the grocery circulars, on the grounds that one cannot know in advance which document will matter.

**Blind spots:** Treats every implication of his work as somebody else's department. He did not consider that forwarding a column to a man who writes exactly like its author might be received as anything other than a courtesy. He checks facts in adjacent realities, including the deaths of their authors, as routinely as he checks the temperature of the instrument room.

**Useful for:** Any story involving the multiverse, parallel selves, nature versus nurture, or the question of what a person would have been under other circumstances. A standing source for documents and data from adjacent realities, delivered without comment.
