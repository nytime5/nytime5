## Public Biography

**Gary Neuhoff**, 58, is a commercial heating and cooling estimator and a resident of [Ypsilanti, Michigan](/wiki/places/ypsilanti/), where he has lived on Prospect Street since 1998. He has worked since 2004 for a mechanical contractor in Van Buren Township, where his job is to convert a building — a thing that exists all at once — into a sequential document that a stranger will read from the top. He is regarded within the firm as unusually good at this.

Mr. Neuhoff was enrolled in 2023 as a subject in the [Center for Discursive Throughput](/wiki/organizations/center-for-discursive-throughput/)'s federally funded study of political speech, in which his complete political position was elicited in writing over eleven sessions and recorded at 2,900 propositions. He has not completed a political sentence in the presence of another adult since October 2011, when a ninety-second exchange in a neighbor's driveway concerning an Ypsilanti Community Schools operating millage was repeated, accurately and in the order given, to a third party, who drew from it a conclusion Mr. Neuhoff does not hold.

He does not characterize the subsequent fourteen years as a silence. He characterizes them as a decision about ordering.

Since March 2026 he has carried in his wallet a laminated card bearing a ninety-one-word summary of his political position, prepared at this newspaper's request by [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/) of the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/). He has handed the card to eleven people. He has not read it aloud to anyone and does not intend to.

Mr. Neuhoff is married, has two adult children, and votes in every election.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Gary Neuhoff is not a crank and is not shy. He is a competent middle-aged professional who has correctly diagnosed a structural problem and adopted a rational workaround, and who is mildly puzzled that this is being written about. He answers questions directly and briefly. His restraint is not emotional; it is procedural, in the way that a man who has read the specification does not argue with the inspector.

**Verbal tics:** Understates. Says "that's about the size of it." Talks about conversation in the vocabulary of his trade — load, capacity, run, throw, "you can't get that through a four-inch duct." Answers a question about his opinions by handing over the card, which he does without ceremony and without apparent expectation.

**Obsessions:** The order of things. The 2011 driveway conversation, which he can reconstruct clause by clause and has, on request, twice. The distinction between being misquoted and being accurately quoted into a position you do not hold — the second being, in his account, worse and more common. The rent in Ann Arbor.

**Blind spots:** Does not consider himself an unusual person, and is therefore a poor judge of what about his situation is newsworthy. Assumes everyone carries something like the card. Believes the study is about the study's other subjects.

**Tone:** Flat, patient, entirely unaggrieved. He has been at peace with this for years.

**Function in stories:** Neuhoff is the standing everyman for stories about the gap between an inner position and its transmissible version — political silence, the collapse of nuance, the citizen who is fully engaged and entirely quiet. He is also available for any Ypsilanti story requiring a resident who holds, without contest, a private conviction the neighborhood has elected not to litigate. He will not raise his voice and he will not be drawn, but he will hand you something.
