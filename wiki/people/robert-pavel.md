## Public Biography

**Robert Pavel**, 67, is a retired letter carrier in [Wausau, Wisconsin](/wiki/places/wausau/), and one of the more than 1.4 million Americans whose presentations led the American Psychiatric Association to add Patriotic Derealization Syndrome, designated 309.94, to the latest revision of its diagnostic manual. In late March 2026 he presented at a local emergency department with what physicians first logged as a possible stroke. He was alert and oriented, could name the day of the week and the current president, and could recite his home address. He could not locate the country he had spent his life in. "It's just gone," he told the resident on call. "It was here last Tuesday."

Mr. Pavel was kept for three days of observation at St. Vincent's Hospital and discharged with a diagnosis of P.D.S., a follow-up appointment, a prescription for low-dose sertraline and a pamphlet titled "When the Country You Loved Feels Different: A Guide for You and Your Family." He [told *The New York Time5*](/2026-05-05/patriotic-derealization-syndrome-america-i-love-does-not-exist/) in May that he had not opened the pamphlet. "I'm not going to read a pamphlet about my own country," he said. "I just want to know where it went." The diagnosis he received is championed by [Dr. Constance Halbritter](/wiki/people/dr-constance-halbritter/) of the [Bethesda Center for Civic Affective Disorders](/wiki/organizations/bethesda-center-for-civic-affective-disorders/) and disputed in principle by [Dr. Reginald Fortenberry](/wiki/people/dr-reginald-fortenberry/), who regards it as a cognitively appropriate response to verifiable change.

He was born in 1959 and lives on the east side of Wausau, three doors from [Duane Ziebell](/wiki/people/duane-ziebell/), whom he has known for about thirty years. In November 2026, asked about Mr. Ziebell's inability to name his own generation, Mr. Pavel said he had never had the slightest difficulty on the question. "I'm a boomer. Born in '59. I have always known which one I'm in," he [said](/2026-11-06/man-born-1961-no-idea-what-intergenerational-groups-are/). "It's the country I can't find."

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Steady, literal and entirely lucid. Robert Pavel carried the mail for a living and approaches the loss of his country the way he would approach an undeliverable address: the house number is right, the street is right, and the house is not there. He is not distressed so much as professionally affronted. He is certain of everything else.

**Verbal habits:** Short declaratives. Speaks of the country as a thing that has been misplaced rather than changed ("where it went," "can't find"). Gives his birth year when asked anything about himself.

**Core contrast:** He and his neighbor [Duane Ziebell](/wiki/people/duane-ziebell/) have opposite problems. Mr. Pavel knows exactly which generation he is in and cannot find the country. Mr. Ziebell can find everything on his old plow route and cannot find his generation. Neither has offered the other help, and neither regards his own problem as the stranger one.

**Open threads:** The unopened pamphlet. The follow-up appointment. Whether the country turns up.

**Avoid:** Making him confused, disoriented or unwell in any sense other than the diagnosed one. Every neurological measure is normal. Do not have him find the country.
