## Public Biography

**Phil Amato**, 67, is a retired housing inspector and a resident of East Ridgewood Avenue in [Paramus, New Jersey](/wiki/places/paramus/), where he has lived since 1988 in a split-level ranch next door to [Dennis P. Hagerty](/wiki/people/dennis-p-hagerty/). He served thirty-four years with the Borough of Paramus Department of Code Enforcement, retiring in 2024, and by his own estimate completed some eleven thousand residential inspections, the majority of them conducted alone in crawl spaces, utility closets and unfinished basements.

Mr. Amato first appeared in *The New York Time5* in March 2026, retrieving his recycling bins from the curb and offering a measured assessment of his neighbor's decision to purchase a second bidet after the first perforated him. He is better known for a September 2026 feature documenting a change in his long-standing habit of talking to himself. For roughly thirty-five years the practice was conversational — Mr. Amato would pose himself a question, consider it, and answer, and the neighbors on East Ridgewood Avenue regarded the audible back-and-forth as a feature of the block. Beginning in the fall of 2025, it became a series of uninterrupted rants, of between eleven and forty minutes, on subjects including the northbound jughandles of Route 17, the self-checkout lanes at the local ShopRite, out-of-state shoppers unaware of the borough's Sunday blue laws, and a deck on the next street whose balusters he estimates, from the sidewalk, at four and five-eighths inches apart.

Since June 2026 he has been seeing [Dr. Judith Fenn-Caraway](/wiki/people/dr-judith-fenn-caraway/), a Ridgewood clinical psychologist who specializes in marital communication, at the urging of his daughter. Dr. Fenn-Caraway completed his intake on her couples form, twice.

His garage, which contains a pegboard of forty-one hand tools hung inside marker outlines of themselves, a workbench, and a folding chair, is regarded on the block as exemplary.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Phil speaks to other people the way a code inspector speaks to a homeowner — courteous, brief, noncommittal, and fully aware that anything he says may later be quoted back to him. All of the heat is reserved for himself. He is polite, even gentle, with neighbors and reporters, and describes his own rants with the detached accuracy of a man writing up a violation he has personally observed. He refers to himself in the first person in both roles ("I don't let me finish"), and does not register this as unusual.

**Obsessions:** Plumb lines, baluster spacing, the four-inch sphere rule, and any piece of built infrastructure that is not what the drawings said it would be. The Route 17 jughandles, which he regards as a design failure the state has chosen to live with. The blue laws, which he defends with the fervor of a lifelong Paramus resident and which supply him with an inexhaustible class of offenders: people who did not know. Having spent a career empowered to cite whatever he found, he now finds a great deal and can cite none of it.

**Blind spots:** Declines every interpretation of his situation larger than the object in front of him. Offered a theory about retirement, marriage, national discourse or the loss of a shared project, he will answer with the name of a specific fixture. He does not see that the rants began after the citations stopped, and would not accept the connection if it were drawn for him.

**Useful for:** Stories set on East Ridgewood Avenue or elsewhere in Paramus; stories about retirement, code enforcement, self-talk, or the deterioration of a long relationship in which both parties are the same person. He is a natural source for anything involving residential construction standards, which he will discuss calmly with anyone but himself. The unresolved question of whether he answers himself again is a standing thread.
