## Public Biography

**Devon Achterberg** is a postdoctoral researcher in theoretical physics at the California Institute of Technology, where he works in the group of [Dr. Anders Ruhl](/wiki/people/dr-anders-ruhl/) on discrete approaches to quantum gravity. He received his Ph.D. from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 2023.

Mr. Achterberg is the discoverer of what has been called the Ruhl correspondence: the observation, made privately over the course of two years, that his adviser's 1,100-page personal manuscript reproduces, in sequence and without attribution, the central results of early modern philosophy. He took an undergraduate ethics course as a sophomore, kept the paperback, and is by his own admission the only member of the research group who would have recognized what was on the chalkboard. He has maintained a private spreadsheet cross-referencing the Framework against the philosophical literature since 2023. It is the document that eventually reached the [Center for Computational Epistemology](/wiki/organizations/center-for-computational-epistemology/) and produced the first formal audit of an independent derivation of an entire discipline.

He has not told Dr. Ruhl about the spreadsheet, and Dr. Ruhl, who does not read philosophy, has not encountered the coverage.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Devon Achterberg is a fundamentally loyal person in an impossible position. He admires his adviser without reservation and has watched him spend eleven years walking, at full speed and with real skill, in a large circle. He is not a whistleblower and does not think of himself as one; he thinks of himself as a man who noticed something and could not stop noticing it. He speaks about the Framework with genuine affection and visible fatigue.

**Verbal tics:** Prefaces corrections with "I want to be fair to him," and then is. Describes his own reactions physically rather than emotionally — he went home, he lay on the floor, he sat in the car for a while. Says "he's not wrong" in a tone that concedes everything and settles nothing.

**Obsessions:** The spreadsheet. It has grown columns — date derived, date originally published, lag in years, whether Dr. Ruhl named it after himself. He updates it the same evening, every time.

**Blind spots:** Believes, without evidence, that the situation will eventually resolve on its own. Has never once considered simply leaving a philosophy book on the desk, because he understands, correctly, that it would not be read.

**Tone:** Wry, tired, protective.

**Assignment notes:** Achterberg is the witness character — the person in the room who knows. Use him whenever a story needs someone who can see the shape of an absurdity from the inside and has decided, out of affection, to keep working there. He is funniest when he is defending the very thing he is exposing.
