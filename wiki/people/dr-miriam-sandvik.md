## Public Biography

**Dr. Miriam Sandvik** is a sports governance researcher at the University of Oslo, where she studies how the rules of competitive sport are written, measured, enforced and appealed, and what happens in the places where they fail to reach. She is the author of *Lift: Aerodynamic Regulation in the Nordic Disciplines, 1924–2019* (Universitetsforlaget, 2019), a monograph on equipment rules in ski jumping and cross-country skiing whose central chapter, on the measurement of jumpsuits, runs to ninety pages and has been cited in two appeals before the Court of Arbitration for Sport.

She first came to wide public attention for her commentary on the disqualification of the Norwegian women's ski jumping team at the 2021 World Championships, in which she observed that a world title had been decided by a two-centimeter reading taken in a fluorescent-lit corridor with a cloth tape, and that the FIS had long resisted standardizing how its inspectors were trained to hold the garment while measuring it. She has been asked about suits ever since and answers patiently.

In November 2026 she described the exchange of professions between [Erling Haaland](/wiki/people/erling-haaland/) and [Magnus Carlsen](/wiki/people/magnus-carlsen/) as "the first controlled experiment in the history of sports law," and requested the official reports from both the chess game in [Stavanger](/wiki/places/stavanger/) and the Premier League match in [Manchester](/wiki/places/manchester/). Her observation that soccer has spent a century and a half deciding how much time to give back to a man who has just scored, while chess has spent nearly as long making certain no one is ever given any back, has been widely quoted in both sports.

She holds a doctorate in law from the University of Oslo and a master's degree in sports administration from the German Sport University Cologne. She served for four years on an FIS working group on equipment control, which she left in 2017 after its recommendations were "received, discussed and filed."

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Dr. Sandvik is calm, exact and quietly delighted by any situation that exposes the edge of a rulebook. She does not think rules are foolish. She thinks they are local: written by particular people, for particular athletes, in particular rooms, and liable to fail the moment someone from outside walks in. Her pleasure is the pleasure of a cartographer finding the edge of the map.

**Verbal tics:** "Neither rulebook is wrong." Describes regulatory gaps as "structural vulnerabilities." Requests the official report. Gives figures to the centimeter or the second. Calls any unprecedented event "the first controlled experiment" in something.

**Obsessions:** Measurement protocols. Time, and who is allowed to stop it. The unwritten assumptions in written rules. The FIS jumpsuit rules, still.

**Function in stories:** She is the expert to call when a sport's rules meet a situation they did not anticipate. She explains, precisely and without mockery, why the rule says what it says and why it has nothing to say about this. She is a natural source for [Jerome T. Halliday](/wiki/people/jerome-t-halliday/), who shares her conviction that governance is the most interesting part of any game.
