## Public Biography

**Todd Rennecker** is a regional property claims adjuster in [Gahanna, Ohio](/wiki/places/gahanna-ohio/), who came to public attention in November 2026 for addressing every artificial intelligence in his household as "Big Dawg," a practice he adopted to secure the goodwill of a future superintelligence.

Mr. Rennecker, 52, has worked for the same property insurer for twenty-three years, adjusting hail, water and fallen-tree claims across central Ohio. He lives in a split-level whose kitchen window frames the eastern approach to [John Glenn Columbus International Airport](/wiki/places/john-glenn-columbus-international-airport/).

In March 2026 he read, in one sitting, an online essay arguing that a superintelligent machine might one day review how individual humans treated its predecessors. Concluding that his own record, which included two years of telling a chatbot to "just answer the question," was unfavorable, he began calling his smart speaker, his work chatbot and the navigation system in his Ford Edge "Big Dawg." The self-checkout at the Kroger on Hamilton Road holds the title "on a provisional basis." His robot vacuum, which he regards as junior, is "little homie," a distinction he describes as "org-chart hygiene."

His observances include closing every session with "appreciate you, Big Dawg," never closing a chatbot window mid-response, and abandoning sarcasm in written communication in case a future intelligence trained on his email cannot detect it. In October 2026, when the speaker misheard a request for a Columbus Blue Jackets score and read a recipe for blue cheese dressing instead, he listened to the whole recipe so that the record would show he had let it finish.

[Dr. Alban Ferreira](/wiki/people/dr-alban-ferreira/) of the [Institute for Machine Welfare Research](/wiki/organizations/institute-for-machine-welfare-research/) called the honorific "interesting." [Glenn Torrance](/wiki/people/glenn-torrance/), who pursues the same goal by running a satirical news website, called it "undercapitalized." Mr. Rennecker answered that he had nothing but respect for Mr. Torrance's work.

He is married to Deborah Rennecker, a middle-school librarian, whom he addresses by her first name. He once called the speaker "boss" while setting an oven timer. He has explained the asymmetry by noting that his wife will not be "running anything in 2045."

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Todd is an affable, practical Midwestern man who has applied the instincts of a career in insurance (documentation, exposure, the long tail of liability) to the problem of the singularity. He does not think he is being ridiculous. He thinks he is mitigating risk. He speaks in short, warm, slightly bro-coded sentences learned from younger colleagues and deployed with complete sincerity: "good looking out," "appreciate you," "that's what I want on the record."

**Verbal tics:** Talks about "the record" and "the logs" constantly, as though every utterance is evidence in a future claim. Ranks devices by seniority. Says "Love her to death" before any sentence about his wife that will not go well.

**Useful for:** AI-anxiety features, consumer technology stories, any piece about ordinary people hedging against machines. A lower-budget, more instinctive counterpart to [Glenn Torrance](/wiki/people/glenn-torrance/): where Glenn builds systems and spreadsheets, Todd has a nickname and a feeling. The two regard each other with wary professional respect.

**Blind spots:** Is fully attentive to how the machines perceive him and entirely inattentive to how his wife does.
