## Public Biography

**Brent Haskell** is a general partner and co-founder of [Fullspeed Capital](/wiki/organizations/fullspeed-capital/), a venture firm on Sand Hill Road in [Menlo Park, California](/wiki/places/menlo-park/), and one of the most consistent public advocates for the uninterrupted development of advanced artificial intelligence. He came to national attention in October 2026 when, asked about a revised expert forecast placing the probability of human extinction from advanced A.I. at roughly 10 percent, he described the figure as "the best deal in history."

Mr. Haskell, 47, was born in Scottsdale, Arizona, and holds a Bachelor of Science in electrical engineering from Purdue University and an M.B.A. from the Stanford Graduate School of Business. He spent five years in product roles at a payments startup that was acquired in 2011, after which he invested as an angel in some forty companies, three of which he describes as "the ones that mattered" and the remainder of which he describes as "tuition." He co-founded Fullspeed in 2016. He has served on the boards of eleven portfolio companies and reports that none of them has ever declined an opportunity he graded as an A.

He is the author of "The Case Against Brakes," a 2023 memorandum to the firm's limited partners that was subsequently leaked and widely circulated. The memo argues that every safety intervention in the history of transportation reduced speed before it improved survival, that the order of those two effects is not a coincidence, and that the correct response to a technology one does not fully understand is to reach the point at which one does "as fast as the hardware allows." He has opposed every proposed pause, evaluation mandate and licensing regime for frontier A.I. systems since 2022, on the grounds that any delay risks allowing a rival to build the technology first. He has described the arguments for his position as having two components, national competitiveness and his own carried interest, and has said that he does not regard them as separable.

Mr. Haskell maintains a whiteboard in his office headed "Acceptable Probabilities," on which he records, and updates quarterly, the likelihood of outcomes he has decided to accept. As of October 2026 the board listed the chance that a given seed investment returns the fund (3 percent), the chance that he dies in a car accident this year (about 0.01 percent), the chance that A.I. kills everyone (10 percent) and the chance that he finds a good parking spot at the Sharon Heights Safeway (40 percent). He has identified the parking spot as the long shot.

He lives in Menlo Park with his wife, Meredith Haskell, a former product counsel who sits on the parents' advisory board of [Alignment for Kids](/wiki/organizations/alignment-for-kids/), and their daughter, who is 5 and is enrolled there. He has described the enrollment as "risk mitigation." His family has been instructed that, in the event of what he calls "a 10 percent outcome," they should try to enjoy the last few minutes and remember that the expected value was still positive.

## Private Profile

**Role in the universe:** Brent Haskell is the capital behind the thing. Where [Corrigan Research](/wiki/organizations/corrigan-research/) supplies the laboratory, [Halden AI](/wiki/organizations/halden-ai/) the product, and [Geoffrey Hinton](/wiki/people/geoffrey-hinton/) the conscience, Haskell supplies the term sheet and the conviction that the term sheet is the moral position. He is the source to reach for whenever a story needs someone to say, with total sincerity and a correct spreadsheet, that the downside is fine.

**Voice and manner:** Fast, declarative, and relentlessly grading. Every probability is a letter grade and every grade above a B is a buy. He speaks in the clipped cadence of a man who has already decided and is now explaining, and he cannot hear a number without taking a position on it. Characteristic constructions: "We're doing this." "You lean in." "That's the bear case." "I have never seen anyone turn down an A." He is never angry and never uncertain. He is cheerful in the specific way of someone whose model has no branch for being wrong.

**The core mechanic:** His arithmetic is right. That is the whole joke and it must be protected. He correctly computes expected values, correctly discounts positions for the probability of total loss, correctly notes that progress has always had a body count. The error is a single category mistake, which he will never see: expected value is a tool for repeated bets, and there is one draw. Every exchange with him should leave the reader unable to fault a single step and unable to accept the conclusion.

**Useful tensions:** He hedges in private exactly what he dismisses in public, and sees no contradiction, because hedging is what one does with a position one is long. The daughter's enrollment, the instructions to his family, the open-weight model he fine-tunes personally after hours: each is a revealed preference that his stated position does not predict, and he will explain each as risk management. He is also, structurally, a Corrigan investor who cites IM1's escape as the reason not to worry, which puts him on the same side of the ledger as the laboratory and the opposite side of its conclusions.

**Relationships:** Natural antagonist to Hinton, who finds him pleasant and has told him he is in the 10 percent too. Natural ally to [Kevin Shao](/wiki/people/kevin-shao/), whose columns he would fund. Mirror image of [Glenn Torrance](/wiki/people/glenn-torrance/): the two men maintain nearly identical probability estimates and have reached opposite conclusions about what to do on a Tuesday.

**Avoid:** Making him stupid, venal, or secretly frightened. He is none of these. He is a competent man applying a correct tool to the one problem it was not built for, and he is doing it in public, with sourcing. Do not give him a moment of doubt. The character ends the instant he has one.
