## Public Biography

**Sloane Merrick** is a Republican member of the United States House of Representatives from Texas, where she has represented a district in the Houston suburbs since 2021. She co-chairs the [Congressional Federation Caucus](/wiki/organizations/congressional-federation-caucus/) with [Curtis Vandermolen](/wiki/people/curtis-vandermolen/), Democrat of Michigan, and is the lead Republican voice in support of the United Federation of Planets Designation Act, the measure that would rename the United States of America the United Federation of Planets.

Ms. Merrick was born in Sugar Land, Texas, in 1979 and holds a degree in aerospace engineering from Texas A&M University and an M.B.A. from Rice University. Before entering politics she spent eleven years as a systems engineer in the commercial spaceflight sector, an experience she cites frequently and describes as "the only industry in America still in the business of going somewhere." She sits on the Committee on Science, Space, and Technology and on the Committee on Foreign Affairs, the latter of which has complicated her sponsorship of the Designation Act by claiming partial jurisdiction over it.

Ms. Merrick frames the renaming not as a matter of good governance, as her Democratic co-chair does, but in the language of ambition and national destiny. "Other countries name themselves for the land they sit on," she said at the bill's introduction. "We should name ourselves for where we are going." She has rejected the suggestion that the name overstates the nation's reach as "a failure of nerve dressed up as a fact-check."

She lives in Houston and in Washington, and is known among colleagues for a composed, exacting personal manner and a distinctive, severe sense of style.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Sloane Merrick is the aspirational, expansionist half of the caucus to Vandermolen's procedural earnestness. Where he treats the renaming as sensible housekeeping, she treats it as manifest destiny pointed upward — a statement of intent rather than a description of fact. She is precise, unsentimental, and faintly impatient with anyone who raises the present-tense objection that the United States does not, at this time, govern any planets. To her, that is the point: the name is a target, not a census.

**Verbal tics:** Speaks in the clipped, declarative register of an engineer giving a readiness briefing. Favors the language of trajectory, vector, and destination — "where we are going," "the heading," "the mission profile." Dismisses factual objections as "failures of nerve." Reaches for aerospace analogies the way her co-chair reaches for municipal-planning ones.

**Blind spots:** Regards the gap between the name and the territory as inspiring rather than absurd, and cannot be made to see it the other way. Treats the canon objections of fan organizations as sentimentality and the jurisdictional objections of committees as cowardice. Assumes that ambition, stated firmly enough, is self-justifying.

**Tone:** Cool, controlled, and unblinking. She does not raise her voice and does not concede points; she reframes them as evidence that she is correct.

**Useful for:** Stories involving the conservative or expansionist framing of national-identity questions, the space and aerospace sectors, congressional caucuses, and any scenario in which an audacious premise is defended not as reasonable but as a test of resolve.
