## Public Biography

**Ingebjørg Thorsrud** is a Norwegian International Arbiter who has officiated chess events across Scandinavia for fifteen years, including the Norwegian national championships and several editions of the elite tournament held each spring in [Stavanger](/wiki/places/stavanger/). She holds FIDE's highest arbiter title, sits on the Norwegian Chess Federation's rules committee, and teaches the federation's arbiter certification course, whose final examination consists of a single game in which the candidate must officiate a match between two instructors who have been told to break the rules in ways the candidate has not been warned about.

She was the chief arbiter for the classical debut of [Erling Haaland](/wiki/people/erling-haaland/) against [Grandmaster Vegard Ulvestad](/wiki/people/vegard-ulvestad/) at the Stavanger Konserthus in November 2026. When Haaland rose without pressing his clock and sat cross-legged on the carpet to celebrate a capture, she stood over the table, one hand raised toward the clock, for the ninety-one seconds until his flag fell. She ruled that his position on the floor did not amount to leaving the playing area, since he remained within a meter of the board and was facing it. "He was present," she said. "He was simply not playing." The event's appeals committee upheld her ruling.

Before the second game of the series, the federation asked whether anything could be done. She advised that Haaland could celebrate for as long as he liked provided he first pressed his clock, so that the time passing was his opponent's. "That is permitted," she said. "That is, in fact, chess."

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Thorsrud is serene, literal and immovable. She has read the Laws of Chess so many times that she no longer experiences them as rules but as physics, and she explains them in the tone of someone describing gravity. She has no opinion about the players. She has the clock.

**Verbal tics:** Short, sequential declaratives that walk the listener to the only possible conclusion ("It knows whose move it is. It was his move. He was on the floor."). Distinguishes carefully between what is permitted and what is wise, and comments only on the first. Ends with a sentence that restates the obvious as though it were a revelation, because to most people it is.

**Obsessions:** The clock. The playing area and its exact boundary. Whether a player is present. The difference between a rule's silence and a rule's permission.

**Function in stories:** She is the universe's ideal official: helpful, precise and incapable of bending. Use her in any story where an outsider meets the rules of chess, or where someone asks an arbiter to exercise discretion she does not have. A natural recurring source for [Jerome T. Halliday](/wiki/people/jerome-t-halliday/).
