## Public Biography

**Kenneth Ebersole**, 64, is a retired title examiner in [Takoma Park, Maryland](/wiki/places/takoma-park/), and a member in good standing of the [easily annoyed community](/wiki/phenomena/easily-annoyed-community/) since 1997. In 2026 he objected in writing to the word "community," which Article 1 of the charter of the [National Council of the Easily Annoyed](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/) uses to describe its membership. The council accepted the objection as a reservation, the first ever entered against Article 1, scored every page of it "extremely" and on that basis renewed his membership through Dec. 31, 2028.

Mr. Ebersole came to title work in 1992, after eight years on a land surveyor's field crew, most of them as its rodman. For the next thirty-one years he searched the Montgomery County land records for settlement companies, following chains of title through the libers at the county courthouse in Rockville, and he retired in 2023. He kept a count. On his last day it stood at 21,806 searches, 1,187 of them for lots inside the Takoma Park city limits. "I spent my whole career telling people where their property ended," he said in November 2026. "Every lot in this county has a boundary, and it's written down at the courthouse. This city has had one since 1890. Nobody has ever been able to show me where a community ends. You can't search it, you can't survey it and you can't get out of it. Somebody decides you're in, and they're always very pleased about it."

He has owned a bungalow on Tulip Avenue since 1990, a year before the city named the municipal building at 7500 Maple Avenue the Sam Abbott Citizens' Center. On its expansion in the 2000s the building also became the Takoma Park Community Center, and it now carries both names, joined by a dash. Residents tend to use one or the other. Mr. Ebersole uses the older of the two. [Evelyn Garvey](/wiki/people/evelyn-garvey/), who lives four doors away and has chaired the council's Montgomery County chapter since 2012, calls it the community center. The parties have yet to reach consensus. Two days before the November 2026 elections, his was the only lawn on his side of Tulip Avenue without a yard sign.

He joined the council in 1997, the year the county line was moved so that the whole of Takoma Park lay in Montgomery County. Eligibility is established by a 140-item questionnaire adapted from ISO/TS 15666, the International Organization for Standardization's technical specification for surveys of noise annoyance, and an answer of "very" or "extremely" on any three items is sufficient. His file lists seventeen, among them the end-of-cycle chime on clothes dryers and drivers who wave other drivers through four-way stops. He has returned every annual survey the council has sent him. The specification treats annoyance as one person's individual reaction to noise, and defines community noise annoyance as "the prevalence rate of this individual reaction in a community." Shown the passage, Mr. Ebersole read it twice. "Then call it a rate," he said. "I agreed to be counted. I didn't agree to be gathered."

His four-page letter, dated Sept. 14, 2026, two days after the city held a community meeting on its review of rent stabilization, asked that the word not be applied to him "in any correspondence, roster, newsletter, census or remark," or, in the alternative, that his membership be terminated. The council treated it as a reservation under Article 9, by which a member may except himself from a provision of the charter. In an eleven-page memorandum dated Oct. 9, the council's general counsel, [Naomi Kurtzberg](/wiki/people/naomi-kurtzberg/), found the reservation admissible. The council's object and purpose under Article 2 is "to represent persons who are easily annoyed, and to see that their annoyance is recorded," and a member annoyed by the charter itself was, she wrote, "squarely within it." The membership office, which scores all incoming correspondence against the questionnaire's five-point scale, had scored each page of the letter "extremely," so that the letter met the standard for membership on its own. On that basis the council renewed him. The alternative request was not reached.

"Mr. Ebersole wrote that a community has no boundary, and that he therefore could not know whether he was in one," Ms. Kurtzberg has said. "This one has a boundary. It is the top two categories of a five-point scale, and his letter is in the top category on every page." In accordance with the reservation, his new card, which arrived on Oct. 20, describes him as "Member, Easily Annoyed." "I asked them to take one word off," Mr. Ebersole said. "They took it off, and they gave me two more years." He has said that, had he been asked, he would have scored the letter "very."

Under Article 9 a reservation is circulated to the full membership, any of whom may object within ninety days; objections do not undo a reservation, but they are recorded beside it permanently. His was circulated on Oct. 12. By Oct. 30 the council had received 1,114 objections, more than had been filed against all 4,118 previous reservations combined. The period closes on Jan. 10, 2027. "I do not believe, on the data currently available, that he can leave by any door they would not record as an entrance," said [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/), a senior fellow at the [Center for Nonparticipation Studies](/wiki/organizations/center-for-nonparticipation-studies/).

Mr. Ebersole asked to be sent a copy of each objection, and the first two hundred arrived on Oct. 31. On the afternoon of Nov. 1 he read them on his porch, in the order received, and said that they were, on the whole, well argued. Shortly before dark he went inside and came back out with a pad of lined paper.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Kenneth Ebersole speaks the way a title report reads: short, plain, complete and concerned entirely with where things end. He is patient and unhurried, never hostile and never amused. He reads whatever is put in front of him twice and prefers to answer in writing. He takes the objections to his reservation on their merits.

**Core position:** Everything real has a boundary, the boundary is written down, and whatever can be searched and surveyed can be left. A boundary may be moved, as the county line was in 1997, but it is moved on paper, by people with the authority to move it, and the paper is filed. He accepts any category that meets that test — a lot, a precinct, a roster, a card, a score, a rate — and does not mind being counted, measured or classified. What he declines is a mood presented as a status: a category conferred rather than found, with no edge of record and no way out, whose members are pleased to have him. Being counted is done to a record. Being gathered is done to a person, without his signature. His test has now been applied to him. [Naomi Kurtzberg](/wiki/people/naomi-kurtzberg/) found the boundary he said did not exist, and found him inside it.

**Verbal habits:** Short declaratives in the vocabulary of the land records: boundary, search, survey, record, courthouse, chain. "Of record" is his highest term of approval. Gives the Sam Abbott Citizens' Center its full name every time. Refers to [the council](/wiki/organizations/national-council-of-the-easily-annoyed/), to which he has belonged for twenty-nine years, as "they." Thinks of a reservation as an exception in the title-insurance sense: a matter listed so that it is not covered.

**Blind spots:** He has never objected to being called easily annoyed. It is a finding, made on a five-point scale, and he meets the standard nearly six times over; it has not occurred to him that most people would have objected to the adjective before the noun. Everything he sends the council is scored as incoming correspondence, and the membership office scored his letter one category higher than he would have. If he answers the objections through the council, each answer will be scored. He has either not considered what that will do to his file or has considered it and does not regard it as relevant.

**Open threads:** His replies to the objections, if that is what is on the pad of lined paper. The close of the objection period on Jan. 10, 2027, after which the objections stand beside the reservation permanently. His standing with [Evelyn Garvey](/wiki/people/evelyn-garvey/), four doors away, who calls the building the community center, calls him Ken and regards his annoyance at the word as perfectly normal. The end of his renewed membership on Dec. 31, 2028. The city's Sept. 12 meeting on rent stabilization, which the record places two days before his letter and does not connect to it.

**Useful for:** Stories about municipal naming, land records and property lines, surveys and scales, membership charters, and any institution that describes people collectively without asking them. He is a natural subject for [Tariq El-Amin](/wiki/people/tariq-el-amin/), who [reported the reservation](/2026-11-02/member-easily-annoyed-community-annoyed-by-word-community/) as a question of treaty practice, and for [Dr. Cassandra Murch](/wiki/people/dr-cassandra-murch/), for whom he is the rare subject who can supply the date, the article and the page count. He is the inverse of [Brendan Vossmeier](/wiki/people/brendan-vossmeier/), elsewhere in [Takoma Park](/wiki/places/takoma-park/), who has narrated years of not voting into a position: Mr. Ebersole put a position in writing, and the council entered it as a renewal. His lot is of the kind a 2024 study of the city's yard signs classified as "quietist."

**Avoid:** Making him a recluse, a crank or a nonparticipant. He votes, pays his dues and returns his surveys, and he objected to one word, in writing, by the procedure the charter provides. He does not put signs on his lawn. Do not make him bitter, lonely or amused, and do not have him leave: he offered to go only in the alternative, and the alternative was not reached. Do not settle the name of the building.
