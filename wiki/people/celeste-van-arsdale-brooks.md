## Public Biography

**Celeste Van Arsdale-Brooks** is the founder and executive director of the [Prosperity Dignity Project](/wiki/organizations/prosperity-dignity-project/), a Washington advocacy organization that promotes person-first language for Americans of substantial means. She describes herself as "a person of wealth with lived experience."

Ms. Van Arsdale-Brooks is a fourth-generation beneficiary of a family trust established in 1911 on the proceeds of a Midwestern freight-car leasing concern. She graduated from Smith College with a degree in comparative literature and spent two decades on the boards of arts institutions before what she has called "an awakening," at a 2021 dinner party, when a fellow guest referred to her within earshot as "one of the rich ones." She founded the project the following year.

In November 2026 she presented the project's style guide at the National Press Club, telling reporters that "a person is not their net worth. Their net worth is something they have. In most cases, it is several things, held through entities." She has said the project is "not asking for anything," only "a different word for what we already have."

She lives in Washington and elsewhere.

## Private Profile

**Voice and sensibility:** Warm, earnest, and fluent in the language of grievance without ever having had occasion to learn it the ordinary way. She speaks in the cadence of a seasoned advocate — measured pauses, first-person testimony, appeals to dignity — and means every word. She is not a hypocrite; she simply cannot see the frame she is standing in.

**Verbal tics:** "Centering." "Our community." Refers to assets as things a person "holds" rather than "has," and to wealth as a "condition." Corrects reporters' terminology mid-question, gently.

**Obsessions:** Being seen as an individual. The visual stereotyping of the wealthy in editorial cartoons. The phrase "tax the rich," which she treats as a slur.

**Blind spots:** Distribution. She has never connected the question of what her community is called with the question of what it pays.

**Function in stories:** The face of wealthy grievance politics. Quotable, sympathetic in manner, and never once self-aware.
